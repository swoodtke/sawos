## Background

Since SO-59, a thread's slot and its Thread-quota row are freed only when the
last reference to it goes. `ThreadOp.Exit` is invoked through a handle naming
the calling thread, which is a counted reference. `Thread.exit(&self)`
(`kernel/sysapi/src/thread.saw`) never returns, so its wrapper never releases
that handle. A typed worker (`thread_self()` then `exit()`) therefore holds its
slot and its quota row until its process dies.

## The ruling

**`Exit` releases the handle it is invoked through**, the way `Reply` consumes
its request handle: the funnel's `consumes` spelling (grep `consumes` in
`kernel/sysapi/src/pipe.saw` and the spec for the idiom).
- Only that one handle goes. Every other handle to the thread (the creator's
  from `thread_create`, any given or sent copy) still references it. The slot
  stays `Exited` and `Join` through those reads the exit code, exactly as today.
- An exited thread nothing else names is freed at once, and its slot and quota
  row come back.

## The change

1. **Kernel** (the `ThreadOp.Exit` arm in `kernel/core/dispatch.saw`,
   `exit_thread` in `kernel/core/sched.saw`): the op releases the invoking
   handle entry, unbinding it from the caller's table and dropping its
   reference, as part of exiting.
   - **Ordering hazard, from the reviewer:**
     - The exiting thread is still `CURRENT_THREAD` until the switch.
       `pick_next`'s deadlock arm reads its `process`, and `exit_thread` reads
       `process` after the wake.
     - So if the drop frees the slot (count reaches zero once it is `Exited`),
       the free must happen **after** the switch away from it, or every reader
       must use a `p` captured before the drop.
     - Pick the design. A deferred free (a "pending free" slot freed by the
       scheduler once the thread is off the CPU) or capturing `p` and dropping
       last are both acceptable. Say which, and trace every read of the
       thread's slot between the drop and the switch.
   - The last-thread path (its exit ends the process) still runs `end_process`
     correctly. The handle being released must not be double-dropped by the
     teardown's close-all; unbind it before the teardown runs.
2. **Typed tier:** `Thread.exit` consumes its wrapper (`move self`, or the
   funnel's `consumes` shape). Update its doc: it releases this handle, the
   others keep the thread readable, and an unnamed exited thread is freed. Fix
   every caller in `root/`, `tests/` and `rt/`. Also update the floor/C doc for
   `sos_thread_exit`.
3. **Carry the two text fixes from the SO-59 approval** (in the issue
   description):
   - spec ~1611–1612, the "a Thread's free crediting nothing" sentence;
   - `free_object`'s docstring (refs.saw ~505) and `credit_shared`'s
     docstring.
   
   Check whether SO-63 already fixed some of them; fix whatever is left.
4. **Spec:** §2's Thread row and §2.10's Bound row (Thread) now state that
   `Exit` releases its handle and an unnamed exited thread is freed at exit.
   Remove anything that says the exited thread holds its slot until its handle
   is released at teardown. No issue numbers; 80 columns.

**Lead review, SO-62.p1.** This implements the user's ruling: `Exit` releases the handle it names.

**Ordering: a deferred free.**
- `exit_thread` marks the thread `Exited`, wakes joiners, captures `p`, unbinds the handle and drops it with `unref_teardown` (count only).
- On the last thread it runs `end_process`. Otherwise, at zero refs it sets `PENDING_FREE`.
- `pick_next` frees the slot after it chooses the next thread, after the deadlock arm, the one reader of `THREADS[cur].process`. `end_process` clears `PENDING_FREE` and its sweep frees the slot.
- The handle is unbound before `end_process`, so the close-all can't drop it twice.

**Typed tier.** `Thread.exit` consumes; 13 test callers moved to `(move me).exit(...)`. Raw-word tests now take two self handles: one is published and one is exited through.

**`handles=`** drops by one where the exiting thread's own handle used to be counted at teardown: `thread_basics` 10→8, `thread_give` 7→6, `thread_quota` 5→4, `thread_give_returns` 10→9.

**Probe (old kernel).** Root's churn ran out of thread slots, and a child refused its 3rd worker at quota.

**Test `thread_exit`.**
- The creator keeps its handle and joins 71 after the worker's consumed exit (the user's required case).
- Root churns 20 unnamed workers against a 7-slot slab.
- A child churns 8 against a quota of 4, then its last-thread exit ends the process cleanly.
- A deadlock role pins the deferred free's correctness. Freeing eagerly blames the deadlock on the wrong process.
- Both mutants are caught: release removed, and an immediate free.

Approved patch SO-62.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SO-62.p1, approved.** It implements the user's ruling, and the deferred free is sound. I read the kernel at a65143e + p1.
- **`exit_thread`** captures `p`, unbinds the word it exited through, drops it with `unref_teardown` (count only), and sets `PENDING_FREE` only at zero. On the last thread, `end_process` runs instead, clears `PENDING_FREE`, and its sweep frees the `Exited` slot. The handle is unbound first, so the close-all can't drop it again.
- **`pick_next` frees the pending slot on every path an exit can reach:**
  - After `Exit`, `CURRENT_THREAD` is the `Exited` thread, so neither early return (`cur == NO_THREAD`, `stays_running`) is taken.
  - The deadlock arm reads `THREADS[cur].process` BEFORE the free and faults into `end_process`, which clears `PENDING_FREE`.
  - The idle branch services interrupts only (no user thread runs, and no ISR path exits a thread or ends a process) and then reaches the free.
  So no second exit can overwrite a pending slot, and nothing can re-reference an `Exited` slot between the exit and the free.
- **A thread holding a second self handle** keeps its count at 1 after the exit and isn't freed. The credit at free goes to the live owner (SO-59).
- **`thread_exit` pins the user's required case:** the creator keeps its handle and joins 71. It also covers root churning 20 unnamed workers against a 7-slot slab, a child churning 8 against a quota of 4, and the deadlock role, which catches an eager free blaming the wrong process.
- **The `handles=` drops** (10→8, 7→6, 5→4, 10→9) are the exiting threads' own handles no longer counted at teardown.
