## Description

**A real allocator in userspace.** Today sosrt's `__saw_rt_alloc` bumps a cursor through the process's arena (a `.bss` region sized by the declaration's `arena`) and `__saw_rt_dealloc` does nothing, so a long-running process that allocates in a loop (a formatted `print`, a `String`, a growing `Vector`) leaks until the arena is exhausted and it panics. The owner (Nov 17): schedule a real allocator in two phases.

**Phase 1 (build first): reclaim within the arena.** A real allocator over the same `.bss` arena: `dealloc` returns memory, freed blocks coalesce, alignment as the ABI asks, check-free where the panic path needs it, and safe with several threads (an atomic or a lock the runtime owns; SO-141 makes the runtime's locks real). Exhaustion stays the panic it is today, with its own exit code (see SO-124's runtime band). Choose the algorithm (a free list with coalescing, size classes, or TLSF) for small code and bounded time; say why. Cases: a process that allocates and frees in a loop far past its arena's size and runs to its end; fragmentation and coalescing at the bounds; exhaustion still caught; every check red when removed (the review standard).

**Phase 2 (design first, for the owner): more memory when the arena runs out.** The arena grows from memory someone else gives: the owner suggests the launcher. To design: who holds the memory (a `Pool` the launcher gives the process at start, which the process allocates `Memory` from and maps itself; or a request to the launcher over a connection, which answers with a `Memory` handle); how the allocator takes a new region (mapped, then added to the free lists); limits (a declaration's ceiling, the pool's budget, quota rows); what a process does when the launcher refuses. Spec changes, if any, go to the owner.

Gate on the implementer's machine against a baseline of f42419e6: sos-test 1126 → 1130 (`arena_reuse` ×3, `arena_churn`), C3 7 and S3 140 (5 board-only) identical; with sizes and numbering masked, the only transcript lines that differ are the four new rows, their image lines and the totals. **Images:** 3860 → 3873 hashed (13 new), 3734 moved (every image that links the allocator), 126 identical (images whose link drops it). Process images grow by about 1.2 KB (riscv32, flat), about 1.5 KB (xtensa), 0 on arm64 (page-padded); a few stock-root riscv32 images grow more (layout padding). **Kernels:** the kernel never calls the allocator at run time, but its link already held the bump `rt_alloc` (reachable from std's `StringBuilder.reserve_bytes` and `String`'s drop glue) and now holds the heap: `.text` +1156 B (riscv32, flat), +1272 (arm64), +1208 (C3), +1428 (S3); `.rodata`, `.data` and `.bss` unchanged.

**Algorithm: TLSF** (`rt/common/src/heap.saw`, module `sosrt.heap`; `lib.saw` keeps the two seams). Free in constant time; a request in constant time except one list walk when no list is certain to hold a fit (without it a request near the arena's size, such as the stock server's 68 KiB in 72, would be refused); freed blocks merge both ways through boundary tags; about 1.3 KB at `-Oz` on riscv32. Blocks are 2-word multiples with a 1-word header (4 words at least); the control block sits at the arena's front, made on the first call; each region ends in a sentinel. A module-private `add_region` carves the first region and is the hook for phase 2 (a later region joins the same way, up to the largest size class the control block was sized for). No compiler check survives in the heap or the two seams (disassembled on riscv32, arm64, xtensa), but the out-of-memory message's `c_string()!`, after the lock is released.

**Thread safety: an `Atomic<Int>` compare-and-swap spin lock in `lib.saw`,** released before the exhaustion message; nothing in its window traps or allocates, so the panic path never meets it held. Not `std.spinlock.SpinLock`: sosrt is compiled into every ESP32-C3 process, which builds without the A extension, and naming `SpinLock` there is refused (`atomics.target`); `Atomic<Int>` works there through `support.c`'s `__atomic_*_4`. On the C3 the lock holds only while nothing preempts inside the routine (the bump cursor's caveat, spec updated); a contended spinner burns its slice (SL-1159's spin-then-yield and SO-141's real locks replace it).

**Decisions where the brief was silent:** `dealloc` reads the size from the block's header, not its arguments (a double free is not detected); exhaustion unchanged (`sos: out of arena memory`, status 65); every alignment honoured, powers of two or not; spec.md's three bump-cursor passages updated as facts (no behaviour change: the owner may want to read them); `arena_race` takes 3000 blocks per worker, not 11000 (a 4-byte request is a 16-byte block now), its expected lines unchanged; `arena_churn` at 4000 rounds (about 1.4 s) after a hang under a loaded gate at 6.7 s.

**Cases:** a host test (`rt/common/tests/heap.saw`, in `HOSTED_TESTS`): regions too small and misaligned with fences, a heap over garbage, 100k rounds, all 120 orders of freeing five blocks merging back into the first, the list walk, the split boundary, alignments 0–1024 and 3, 12, 24, 48, 96, refusals including wrapping alignments, 200k random steps checking every live block. `arena_reuse` (three profiles): 2000 KiB of vectors through an 8 KiB arena to a clean exit; three quarter-arena blocks freed out of order, then a three-quarter request served at the first's address; alignment to 1024 and the gaps merged back. `arena_churn` (riscv32, short tick): two preempted threads allocating and freeing with markers, then a three-quarter request served. `arena_small` (exhaustion) unchanged.

**Mutants:** heap (host) 45 of 46 red; the 46th (`step < grain()`) only lets the compiler drop a divide check, and removing it brings `__saw_fail_divide` back into the object (red in the machine code). Requested changes on patch SO-142.p1 (revision 1)

**Changes requested, for the revision the lock is getting anyway. The heap itself holds under everything thrown at it; what follows is about misuse, what the hosted test does not reach, and three statements.** An Opus agent read the heap, wrote a stress harness and hosted mutants; I read `heap.saw` and `lib.saw`, ran everything named here again, and ran the emulator side.

**1. A double free, or a free of something `alloc` did not hand out, now damages the heap; the note says only "not detected".** Under the bump arena `dealloc` did nothing, so neither could do harm before.

- **A block freed twice** (f367): the next three requests of its size are all given that block. With the heap full, a request that walks its list never returns, and in `__saw_rt_alloc` that is inside the lock, so the process's other threads spin with it.
- **A pointer into a live block** (f368): with a size-like word before it, the inside of the live block is served again; with a zero word, the heap writes outside its region (a segfault on the host with other bytes there).
- **Nothing in the gate does either today.** I ran the whole gate on a heap that refuses such a free with a line and a status and fills every served and every freed block (f366): 1,130, C3 7, S3 140, all green, the line never printed. So today's programs neither free badly nor read what they did not write.
- **For the owner:** an immediate double free is one compare (the header's `FREE` bit), a stray pointer three. Stop the machine with a status of its own, as exhaustion does, or leave it and say in `rt_dealloc`'s and `free`'s docs what happens.

**2. The hosted test never reaches the table's top class, its last list or the class one past it.** 23 of the agent's 67 hosted mutants pass (f369); twelve assertions close fourteen of them (f370; rerun by me, green on the patch and each red with its mutants).

- **Among the fourteen:** the list heads laid over the top class's map, the last head not zeroed, and `fl <= classes`, each a segfault once its assertion runs.
- **Why they pass now:** every check with real work in it uses a 4096 or 16384 byte region, where no block reaches the top class. The real arenas (72K, 96K, 48K) are not powers of two, and their first block lives there.
- The other nine are equivalent, each with its premise in the list.

**3. Statements.**
- **`lib.saw`:** "nothing in a kernel image calls the two seams below and its link collects them". A kernel image holds `__saw_rt_alloc`, `__saw_rt_dealloc`, the heap and `ARENA_LOCK` (`llvm-nm` on riscv32's `clock_basics.elf`), as your note says.

- **The agent's reading, not rechecked by me:** `arena-race`'s source says both workers' blocks fit the arenas its scripts set, which the arm64 script's does not (the case runs on riscv32 only); `lib.saw` lists "a formatted `print`" among what allocates; `heap.saw`'s "the largest a nearly full region has" describes the walk's case backwards (it is a nearly empty arena, 68 KiB of 72).
