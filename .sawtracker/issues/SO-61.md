## The pattern to follow

SO-59 (threads) and SO-58 (Events) have merged; read both commits first.
- **SO-59** is the bound-kind pattern: an invalid state, `invalidate_*` at the
  owner's death, `Revoked` answered after the dispatch checks, freed at zero,
  disowned at teardown, and list hygiene before invalidating.
- **SO-58** is the givability pattern: `Transfer`, the `BootHandleKind`,
  `msg_kind_of`, and the typed give, carry, decode and accessor.

Waiters and Timers are **bound** kinds (spec §2.10's table). Do for each what
SO-59 did for Threads, adapted below.

## The contract (spec.md; read first)

- §2.10:
  - the Bound row;
  - "An op on an invalid object answers a status";
  - "What every op covers";
  - "An attachment on an invalidated waitable fires" (a terminal level,
    removable by key; the record is the kind's ordinary record);
  - the teardown order;
  - the disown rule;
  - the refund rule (a bound kind's charge is refunded at invalidation).
- §2.2: a Waiter destroyed under parked threads wakes them with `Revoked`.
  This already happens at a free (`revoke_blocked`) and must now also happen
  at invalidation.
- §2.6, Timers: arm, disarm, fire, and what a Timer frees.

## The change

1. **Givable**, both kinds, in SO-58's shape:
   - `waiter_rights()` and `timer_rights()` gain `Transfer`;
   - `BootHandleKind.Waiter` and `.Timer`;
   - `msg_kind_of`;
   - the typed `PipeHandle` cases, the carrier, `decode_handle`,
     `Process.give(waiter:/timer:, tag:, keep:)` with the hand-back, and the
     `BootHandle` accessors.
2. **The invalid state.**
   - The owner's death invalidates every Waiter and Timer it owns that is still
     referenced elsewhere.
   - Ones referenced only by the dying table are simply freed, as today.
   - Choose the representation, a state value per kind as SO-59 did for Thread,
     and say why. SO-59's note says adding `Invalid` to the shared `SlotState`
     would touch every exhaustive match; decide whether these two kinds need
     their own enum or a flag.
3. **Invalidating a Timer:** disarm it, so it never fires again and the
   comparator is reprogrammed, then refund its charge and disown it. An
   attachment on it (another process's Waiter) **fires** as an invalidated
   waitable: its record is the Timer's ordinary record, a terminal level. Every
   later op on the Timer answers `Revoked`: `Arm`, `Disarm` and a wait.
4. **Invalidating a Waiter:**
   - Wake every thread parked on it with `Revoked`. They belong to other
     processes, since the dying process's own threads are already invalidated.
   - Detach its attachments, dropping their references to the waitables, as a
     free would.
   - Refund its charge and disown it.
   - Every later op (`Add`, `Remove`, `Wait`) answers `Revoked`.
   - Check the deadlock predicate and the blocked-list invariants.
5. **Freed at zero:** an invalid Waiter or Timer is freed when its count reaches
   zero, by a release or by a teardown's sweep, whichever comes last. That
   includes another dying process's close-all dropping the last name, as SO-59
   handles for threads.
6. **Teardown order and counts:** follow §2.10's order.
   - The by-owner sweeps change from "free everything owned" to "free what
     nothing else names; invalidate and disown the rest".
   - The `waiters=` and `timers=` fields keep counting the dying process's own
     objects that the teardown freed. Say so in §2.10, as SO-58 and SO-59 did.
7. **Spec:** remove every marker this satisfies for Waiter and Timer:
   - §3 `Transfer` and boot kinds;
   - §2.1 message kinds;
   - §2.7;
   - §2.2's owner-death wake marker;
   - §2.10's invalidation, attachment-fires and teardown items;
   - §11.
   
   Edit mixed markers down to what is still unbuilt (Mapping, Interrupt, the
   `Gone`-process `Revoked` answers, `MINT_OP` on invalid objects).

## Tests (probe first)

Model them on SO-59's `thread_give` and SO-58's `event_*` cases:
1. **Give and send:** a Waiter and a Timer given at boot and carried in a
   message are both usable, and a refused give hands each back.
2. **Timer invalidated:** a child arms a Timer and gives it to root; root
   attaches it to its own Waiter. The child dies. Root's attachment fires (root
   waits and gets the Timer's record), and every later op on the Timer answers
   `Revoked`. The Timer never fires again: wait past its deadline and show no
   second delivery for a periodic timer.
3. **Waiter invalidated:** a child creates a Waiter and gives it to root; a root
   thread parks on it. The child dies, and root's thread wakes with `Revoked`.
   Later ops answer `Revoked`. Attachments it held drop their references; show
   an Event attached to it is freed once released, or can be attached to
   another Waiter.
4. **Freed at zero:** capacity comes back after the last handle goes, counting
   creates until refused.
5. **Refund:** the over-credit probe in SO-58's shape. The dead owner's record
   is reused, and a later free of the invalid object credits nobody.

Record today's behaviour for each in `<scratchpad>/INV2/probe-before.txt`.
Mutation-check the invalidation calls and the attachment firing.

**Lead review, SO-61.p1: Waiters and Timers are givable, and invalidated at their owner's death.** It follows SO-59's pattern.

**Representation.** Each kind has its own `WaiterState` and `TimerState` (`Free`/`Live`/`Invalid`, with `Free = 0`), so every match had to say what `Invalid` means.

**Teardown.**
- `unblock_process(p)` runs before the close-all and takes `p`'s threads off every Waiter's blocked list. It replaces "empty `p`'s own lists".
- `invalidate_waiter` wakes the other processes' parked threads with `Revoked`, refunds and disowns.
- `invalidate_timer` disarms, refunds, disowns, then `notify_ready`, so an attachment on another process's Waiter fires as a terminal level. Its record carries `fires=0`.
- The sweeps free what nothing names, invalidate `p`'s still-named objects and free invalid ones at zero. Frees credit through `credit_shared`.

**Ops on an invalid object.** `Add`, `Remove`, `Wait`, `Arm` and `Disarm` answer `Revoked` after the dispatch checks. `ReplyWait`'s wait leg answers `Revoked`/`Wait`. `MINT_OP` is unchanged, as marked.

**Outside the brief.** With givable Waiters, a dying process's Interrupt can be attached to another process's Waiter. The Interrupt sweep now detaches it before the free and runs after the attachment sweep. It doesn't fire yet; Interrupt invalidation is the next unit, as marked.

**Probe.** On today's kernel every give faults. With `Transfer` added but no invalidation, objects leak and root deadlocks.

**Tests.** `waitable_give`, `give_waitable_returns`, `timer_outlive` (fires=0 terminal, `Revoked` ops, no later fire, refund probe) and `waiter_outlive` (a parked wait woken `Revoked`, the Event moves on, refund probe). Each mutation breaks the case it targets.

**Runs.** The full suite passes locally (490 runs), plus the C3 smoke. No existing assertion changed.

Requested changes on patch SO-61.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SO-61.p1, changes requested: a userspace-reachable double drop (with a sibling already on main), and a new strand in `ReplyWait`.** I re-read the code for both. The rest is sound: counting, credit-once and the Invalid ops. The body-free halt (SO-54/59) stays unreachable: every post-close-all delivery source was enumerated.

**1. Double drop of an attachment's reference, which a program can trigger. NEW in this patch, and the same mechanism already exists on main.**
- **The new route (the attachment sweep, sched.saw ~547-568).** For each of p's Waiter's rows the sweep frees the row, sets `WAITERS[owner].attached = 0` and drops the reference (`unref_waitable_teardown`). That drop can raise a level: `drop_reference` notifies a pipe end.
- Because Waiters are now givable, ANOTHER process's thread can still be parked on p's Waiter at that point. `unblock_process` removes only p's threads, and `invalidate_waiter` runs later, in the Waiter sweep.
  1. The raised level wakes that thread through a row of the same Waiter the sweep hasn't reached (`wake_one_waiter`).
  2. If the delivery ends the attachment (one-shot, `PipeReply`), `deliver_attachment` calls `detach_attachment(w, a)`.
  3. `detach_attachment` walks from the zeroed head, finds nothing, and leaves the row Live with its back pointer set, while `unref_waitable` drops its reference.
  4. The sweep then reaches that row and drops the same reference again.
- **A concrete halt.**
  1. p gives Waiter W to B.
  2. B posts and takes (claim r, obligation q).
  3. B attaches r to W consuming (row A1), attaches q one-shot keeping its q handle (row A2 > A1), and parks on W.
  4. p exits. At A1 the claim drop notifies the request side, B is woken through A2, and the detach misses. The request column goes 2→1 at the delivery and 1→0 at the sweep, while B still holds q.
  5. The reap settles the exchange.
  6. B's reply through q hits `fatal_kernel("a pipe obligation named a free exchange")` (`pipe_reply_status`). Other variants reach `pipe_resolve`'s Free-claim halt, or a stale handle onto a reused slot.
- **The route already on main: `free_object`'s Waiter cascade (refs.saw).** It walks and frees rows without updating `WAITERS[slot].attached`, then calls `revoke_blocked` AFTER the cascade. A thread still parked on the Waiter can be handed a delivery mid-cascade, and its `detach_attachment` walks from the freed head (link 0), misses the later row, and the cascade drops it again. The comment there ("The order is not load-bearing … the wake reads no attachment") is wrong. On main it's reachable by ONE process with two threads (T1 parked on W, T2 releases W's last handle, with W attached to objects whose drop raises another of W's rows). This patch adds more routes (the Waiter sweep freeing another process's Waiter, staged releases freeing p's Waiters).
- **Fix, at both sites:** answer the parked threads BEFORE tearing the list down. Then `wake_one_waiter` returns at `blocked == 0` and no delivery can run mid-walk.
  - In `free_object`, call `revoke_blocked(slot)` before the cascade.
  - In `end_process`, revoke the threads parked on p's Waiters before the close-all (step 1, "invalidate first").
  - Alternatively, have the walks unlink each row through `detach_attachment` so the list stays consistent.
- Please add a case for each site. The main-side one probably deserves its own issue, so it's fixed even if this unit moves.

**2. A strand in `ReplyWait`: a thread can park on a Waiter its own reply just freed (new, now that Waiter handles carry `Transfer`).**
- `check_send` refuses the invoking word as a carried handle but not `args.waiter`.
- `pipe_reply_wait` resolves `w` (`reply_wait_waiter`), and then the reply leg can carry w's last handle. If the client is a fused `Call` whose table is full, `wake_call_reply` refuses for good, the exchange settles, and `release_staged` frees the Waiter (state Free).
- `waiter_invalid` answers `false` for `Free`. With no attachments the thread goes to `block_on_wait` on a Free slot: nothing wakes it, and the next `waiter_create` reusing the slot drops it from the list.
- It's the caller's own thread, so it isn't a use-after-free, but it breaks "nothing parked can be silently doomed". The typed API can't express it; a raw syscall can.
- Fix: fault `BadArg` when `args.waiter`'s handle is among the carried handles, or re-check after the reply leg with `Free` answered `Revoked`.

**3. The Interrupt detach without a fire is marked as intended (spec ~1525-1528, ~563-565).** The code comment at sched.saw ~576-579 should say so too.

**4. Tests: these mutants survive:**
- (a) `unblock_process` skipping other processes' Waiters. No case kills a process while its thread is parked on another process's Waiter, and the real effect is severe: `invalidate_thread` zeroes `link` mid-list, and a later wake pops an invalid thread.
- (c) `invalidate_timer` not disarming. Every timer walk skips `Invalid`, and the owner arms just before exiting.
- Nothing covers findings 1-2 or the Interrupt detach.

**5. Test comments that are wrong:** `waitable_give` (runner block ~4883; main.saw:34-36, :180-181) says the child's pair is "invalidated by its death … by root's releases". `use_sent` releases both before root drops the request, while the child lives, so `waitable_give` never exercises invalidation. It does cover another process's Waiter and Timer freed by the dying process's sweeps.

**6. Possibly flaky:** `timer_outlive` relies on the owner's 5 ms arm not firing before its exit. A host stall would deliver fires=1 first. Please run it a few hundred times.

**7. Nits:**
- The Timer arm of `waitable_ready` uses `case _`, against the per-kind-enum rationale.
- `WaiterSlot.refs` ("Only handles") and `TimerSlot.refs` omit staged messages, and time.saw ~58-59 ("non-zero … the whole of what a Waiter looks at") is false for an invalid Timer.
- refs.saw ~29-34: the Interrupt sweep now also frees another process's Interrupt.
- spec ~1618-1625: `waiters=`/`timers=` don't list "freed inside the close-all by a staged release", as `events=`/`threads=` do.
- `waiter_add` on an invalid Waiter answers `Revoked` before the one-Waiter check, so a second attach through an invalid Waiter answers `Revoked` instead of faulting. That's consistent with the docstring; flagged only because of the per-object ruling.
