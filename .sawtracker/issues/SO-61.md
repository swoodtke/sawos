## The pattern to follow

SO-59 (threads) and SO-58 (Events) have merged; read both commits first.
- **SO-59** is the bound-kind pattern: an invalid state, `invalidate_*` at the
  owner's death, `Revoked` answered after the dispatch checks, freed at zero,
  disowned at teardown, and list hygiene before invalidating.
- **SO-58** is the givability pattern: `Transfer`, the `BootHandleKind`,
  `msg_kind_of`, and the typed give, carry, decode and accessor.

Waiters and Timers are **bound** kinds (spec §2.10's table). Do for each what
SO-59 did for Threads, adapted below.

- §2.2: a Waiter destroyed under parked threads wakes them with `Revoked`.

## The change

1. **Givable**, both kinds, in SO-58's shape:
   - `waiter_rights()` and `timer_rights()` gain `Transfer`;
   - `BootHandleKind.Waiter` and `.Timer`;
   - `msg_kind_of`;
   - the typed `PipeHandle` cases, the carrier, `decode_handle`,
     `Process.give(waiter:/timer:, tag:, keep:)` with the hand-back, and the
     `BootHandle` accessors.
2. **The invalid state.**
   - The owner's death invalidates every Waiter and Timer it owns that is still
     referenced elsewhere.
   - Ones referenced only by the dying table are simply freed, as today.
   - Choose the representation, a state value per kind as SO-59 did for Thread,
     and say why. SO-59's note says adding `Invalid` to the shared `SlotState`
     would touch every exhaustive match; decide whether these two kinds need
     their own enum or a flag.
3. **Invalidating a Timer:** disarm it, so it never fires again and the
   comparator is reprogrammed, then refund its charge and disown it. An
   attachment on it (another process's Waiter) **fires** as an invalidated
   waitable: its record is the Timer's ordinary record, a terminal level. Every
   later op on the Timer answers `Revoked`: `Arm`, `Disarm` and a wait.
4. **Invalidating a Waiter:**
   - Wake every thread parked on it with `Revoked`. They belong to other
     processes, since the dying process's own threads are already invalidated.
   - Detach its attachments, dropping their references to the waitables, as a
     free would.
   - Refund its charge and disown it.
   - Every later op (`Add`, `Remove`, `Wait`) answers `Revoked`.
   - Check the deadlock predicate and the blocked-list invariants.
5. **Freed at zero:** an invalid Waiter or Timer is freed when its count reaches
   zero, by a release or by a teardown's sweep, whichever comes last. That
   includes another dying process's close-all dropping the last name, as SO-59
   handles for threads.
6. **Teardown order and counts:** follow §2.10's order.
   - The by-owner sweeps change from "free everything owned" to "free what
     nothing else names; invalidate and disown the rest".
   - The `waiters=` and `timers=` fields keep counting the dying process's own
     objects that the teardown freed. Say so in §2.10, as SO-58 and SO-59 did.
7. **Spec:** remove every marker this satisfies for Waiter and Timer:
   - §3 `Transfer` and boot kinds;
   - §2.1 message kinds;
   - §2.7;
   - §2.2's owner-death wake marker;
   - §2.10's invalidation, attachment-fires and teardown items;
   - §11.
   
   Edit mixed markers down to what is still unbuilt (Mapping, Interrupt, the
   `Gone`-process `Revoked` answers, `MINT_OP` on invalid objects).

## Tests (probe first)

Model them on SO-59's `thread_give` and SO-58's `event_*` cases:
1. **Give and send:** a Waiter and a Timer given at boot and carried in a
   message are both usable, and a refused give hands each back.
2. **Timer invalidated:** a child arms a Timer and gives it to root; root
   attaches it to its own Waiter. The child dies. Root's attachment fires (root
   waits and gets the Timer's record), and every later op on the Timer answers
   `Revoked`. The Timer never fires again: wait past its deadline and show no
   second delivery for a periodic timer.
3. **Waiter invalidated:** a child creates a Waiter and gives it to root; a root
   thread parks on it. The child dies, and root's thread wakes with `Revoked`.
   Later ops answer `Revoked`. Attachments it held drop their references; show
   an Event attached to it is freed once released, or can be attached to
   another Waiter.
4. **Freed at zero:** capacity comes back after the last handle goes, counting
   creates until refused.
5. **Refund:** the over-credit probe in SO-58's shape. The dead owner's record
   is reused, and a later free of the invalid object credits nobody.

Record today's behaviour for each in `<scratchpad>/INV2/probe-before.txt`.
Mutation-check the invalidation calls and the attachment firing.
