**The priority scheduler of spec §7, with what networking requires of it.** Today the kernel runs one round-robin FIFO on a one-tick slice. Spec §7 specifies, but does not build, the following:
- fixed-priority preemption over 8 system levels, with a ready bitmap;
- `Priority` bands (Background, Low, Normal, High);
- a per-process band→level map fixed at creation from the image's metadata and the launcher's policy;
- direct switches on post and reply;
- no priority inheritance.

The networking design (`SO:networking`, SO-103, §4.1–4.3 and the reviewer's thread t1) adds three requirements:
- **Levels inside one process:** a driver process holds threads at several levels. The radio's interrupt threads sit above its tasks, and BT's sit above Wi-Fi's.
- **Strict preemption on wake.**
- **A priority ceiling** for the libraries' critical sections. It is a per-process deferred-preemption word that belongs to the process and survives its holder being off the processor (the reviewer's note for this unit).

There is also requirement 4.2, a latency diagnostic.

**First a design (Opus), for the owner and the reviewer.** Nothing is built before both have read it.

The design must also cover the parts of §7 that clash with today's tests. Today `CLAUDE.md` and the case table rely on one FIFO ready list ("a yield returns once every thread that was runnable at the yield has run"). The design says how that ordering survives at one level, and what changes for cases that use it.

It covers every profile: riscv32, arm64, riscv32-flat and the ESP32-S3.

It also covers the tick, idle and deadlock rules, the `Stats` columns, how a level reaches a thread through `thread_create`, and the image, declaration and builder side.

**Lead: the design is written and published as the tracker doc `SO:priority-scheduler`, revision 1.** It changes nothing in the repository. The owner and the reviewer read it before anything is built.

**Verdict.** It builds in five units. One level behaves exactly as today's scheduler, so no existing case's expected text changes.
- **Levels.** 16 system levels, a ready list per level, and a 16-bit bitmap. Pick-next is a compare, a 256-byte table load and a pop. It is arch-free on every profile; riscv32 has no CLZ.
- **Bands.**
  - Each process has 8: spec's four plus `Band4` to `Band7`, held in a one-word map of nibbles.
  - The band rides `ThreadOp.Start`'s unused `arg0`.
  - A launcher sets a child's map with `ProcessOp.BandMap` between create and start, and grants at most its own highest level.
  - A process never names a system level.
- **Preemption on wake.** It is a level compare in `ready_push` that raises `NEED_RESCHED`. The switch stays at `ktrap`'s user return, and a preempted thread goes back to the head of its level.
- **The ceiling is a section word.** It is a depth and pending pair in the process's memory, registered with `ProcessOp.SectionWord`.
  - While the depth is nonzero, only the holder runs among its process's threads, in its siblings' place. Other processes are untouched.
  - Entering and leaving the section don't trap.
  - Parking or exiting inside it faults the process with `SectionHeld`.
  - The radio needs six levels, not seven.
- **No inheritance.** The direct switch comes later, as its own unit; preemption on wake already covers the cross-level case.

**Two findings.**
1. **The runtime's locks livelock once one process runs threads at two levels.** The arena and heap spin, and the output lock yields. U3 moves the heap windows under the section word and parks the output lock on an Event. Until then, threads at two levels must not contend on them.
2. **The networking design's latency bound (§4.3 point 6) is too small.** A wake at a preemption point switches only when the whole operation ends. An always-on latency record (`InterruptOp.Latency`) measures it.

**Units.**
- U1, levels, bands and wake preemption (M, Opus), first.
- U2, the section word (M, Opus).
- U3, the runtime's locks (S), after U2.
- U4, the builder and stock root: `priorities` in `process.toml`, `Child.bands` (M), after U1.
- U5, the latency record (S).
- U6, the direct switch, later.

**Owner's questions**, recommended yes to all:
1. 16 levels, not 8.
2. 8 bands per process.
3. A child's request in `process.toml`, with the header field for root only.
4. The ceiling as a section word.
5. Parking inside a section faults.
6. The section word gated on `ThreadCreate`.
7. A preempted thread resumes at the head of its level.
8. The direct switch later.
9. The latency record always built, read through the line's handle.
10. No new `Stats` columns.
11. The output lock parks on an Event and Waiter made at first contention.
12. The names `Band4` to `Band7`.

**Owner's rulings, Nov 24, on `SO:priority-scheduler` revision 1:**
- **Q1: 8 system levels, not 16.** "let's keep it at 8 priority levels".
- **Q2 and Q12, bands and full access.** "let's allow a process request full access to all 8 priority levels via the process config (instead of the simple mapping which is the default). for the names ..., can we add 4 'realtime' or 'rt' bands?"
  - A process has 8 bands: the four of spec §7 (`Background`, `Low`, `Normal`, `High`) and four realtime bands.
  - By default the launcher maps them with the simple mapping (§7's default map extended to 8).
  - A process whose declaration asks for full access gets its 8 bands mapped one to one onto the 8 system levels. It is still a declaration the image builder checks and the launcher grants, never a raw level named at a syscall.
  - The design proposes the realtime band names; the lead suggests `Rt0` to `Rt3` in Saw and `rt0` to `rt3` in TOML.
- **Q11, the output lock's wait.** The owner asked whether parking on an Event is a priority-inheritance mutex, and whether we should use one instead. The lead's answer: it isn't. Parking ends the spin, but a high thread still waits behind a low holder that a middle thread can preempt. Revision 2 weighs a priority-inheritance lock for the runtime's in-process locks (owner known, one process, so inheritance is well defined, unlike across pipes) against the section word as a ceiling, and recommends one.
- **Q3–Q10:** accepted as recommended.

**Lead: `SO:priority-scheduler` revision 2 is published.** It follows the owner's rulings; its top section lists what changed.
- **Levels and bands.** Eight levels with a one-byte bitmap. The bands are `Background`, `Low`, `Normal`, `High` and `Rt0`–`Rt3`.
  - **The default map** puts `Background` at level 0 and every other band at 1, so every existing case runs at level 1 unchanged.
  - **Full access:** `bands = "full"` in `process.toml` maps band b to level b, within the launcher's own cap.
  - **The radio under full access:** serve thread at 2, Wi-Fi task 3, timer 4, BT controller 5, Wi-Fi interrupt thread 6, BT interrupt thread 7.
- **The priority-inheritance lock.**
  - **Taking and giving.** The lock word is two words: the holder's Thread handle word and a waiters flag. A free lock is taken and given by CAS, with no syscall.
  - **`ThreadOp.LockWait` (4)** raises the holder along a chain of at most 4; a longer chain or a cycle is the `LockChain` fault (16).
  - **`LockWake` (5)** wakes every waiter and lowers the holder.
  - **A holder that exits holding the lock:** its waiters are answered `Revoked`, and the runtime panics.
  - **Who uses which:** the heaps and arena use the section word, which is short and never parks; the output lock uses the inheritance lock, because prints are long and park on the console.
  - **Spec §7's no-inheritance-across-pipes rule stays.**
- **Numbers:** `ProcessOp.BandMap` 16, `SectionWord` 17, `ProcessRight.BandMap` 1<<23, and `InterruptOp.Latency` 1. `band-map` joins SO-105's rights table.
- **Units:** U1 levels; U2 section word; U3 inheritance lock; U4 builder and stock root; U5 runtime locks; U6 latency record; U7 direct switch, later.
- **Owner's questions,** with yes recommended to all:
  1. `bands = simple|full` as a mode;
  2. full access granted as asked, with no image key for now;
  3. a general kernel `BandMap`;
  4. `LockWake` wakes all;
  5. an exiting holder means `Revoked` and a runtime panic;
  6. `LOCK_CHAIN_MAX` 4;
  7. section word for the heaps, the lock for output;
  8. the lock exported as `sos.Lock` and in the C floor, for the radio adapter's FreeRTOS mutexes.
