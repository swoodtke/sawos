**Networking K1s: the quarantine in the stats.**

The design is `SO:virtio-net` revision 4, §3.5.
- **The pool.** `Pool.Available` grows from three words to five: `pinned` and `quarantined`.
- **The process.** The stats row gains a `pinned` doubleword. A dead process's record keeps it, so a supervisor can read what a dead driver left pinned.
- **The sweep.** Every reader of the pool record and the stats row moves to the new sizes.
- **Cases.** `pool_available_pinned`, `process_stats_pinned`, and step 3 of `memory_pin_quarantine`.

Approved patch SO-166.p1 (revision 2)

**Approved, revision 2.** It implements SO:virtio-net r4 §3.5 as written. I ran it on riscv32 with sawlang v0.24.3, on main at 68667106, since 33d0986b is not on origin; the patch applied cleanly.

**Read against the design:**
- `Pool.Available`'s words 3 and 4 come from the pool slot's `pinned_bytes` and `quarantined_bytes`.
- Pin raises the pool's count and the pinner's column.
- The release that finds a pinned object nameless raises `quarantined`, once. Nothing names the slot afterwards, so nothing releases it again.
- Unpin lowers the pool's count, and the pinner's column only while the pinner has its body.
- `stats_clear` zeroes the column for a reused body.
- `quarantined` never falls, which matches §3.4: only a future supervisor could unpin a nameless object.

**The sweep:** every reader of the two records goes through the sysapi types and the constants in `records.saw`, which the patch moves. The design's list (`stats-region`, `stats-region-ro`, `stats-reader`, `pid-stats-row`, `child-buffer-rule`, `map-overlap`, `pool-ops`) has no hard-coded size, and the stats region's length is computed from the row.

**My mutants:** 10 of 10 red, over `pool_available_pinned`, `process_stats_pinned`, `memory_pin_quarantine`, `memory_unpin_returns` and `process_stats`.
- the pool's raise dropped, and its lower dropped;
- the quarantine raise dropped, or counted into `pinned`;
- the column's raise dropped, and its lower dropped;
- the lower charged to the unpinner;
- the `has_body` test dropped (red on the dead-pinner unpin);
- the reused body's clear dropped (red on the second child's record);
- the two words swapped.

`process_stats_pinned` covers the three cases the design's wording leaves easiest to get wrong: an unpin by another process, an unpin after the pinner's death, and a body reused after a pinner's death.
