## Description

## Finding (from SO-90's implementer, Nov 13)

Reasoned from the code; not tried.

A launcher may be able to free the bytes a running child's image lives
in, so that another process can be given them.

## The sequence believed to do it

1. Root runs `process_create(image, memory: dst)`. `place_image` records
   the child's image and stack rows through `record_grant`, which counts
   each row on `dst`'s slot (`ref_region_row`, `MEMORIES[dst].maps`).
2. Root runs `dst.split(len)` (`MemoryOp.Split`). Nothing checks `maps` or
   live rows. `pool_cut` hands the front of `dst` to a new slot `front`,
   whose base is the child's image base, and advances `dst`'s base. The
   child's rows still count against `dst`'s slot, not `front`'s.
3. Root releases its `front` handle while the child lives. `front` has no
   references and no rows counted on it, so `memory_release_if_idle`
   returns its range to the pool's free list.
4. A later split from the pool can hand those bytes to another `Memory`,
   mappable into a third process, while the child still runs from them.

What was observed, in SO-90's own case: after a split the child's rows go
on referencing `dst`'s slot.

## What the spec says

§2.5's second-zero rule: a placed segment and the stack grant are
references on the destination region, so a launcher that releases its
handle while the child runs leaves a husk and the bytes come back when
the child dies. A split that moves live bytes under another slot is not
covered by that count.

## To settle

- A probe: can the sequence be built, on which profiles, and what a third
  process sees.
- The rule: either a `Memory` with rows counted on it cannot be split, or
  a split carries the rows' count to whichever piece covers them.

## Acceptance

- [ ] the probe, as a case if the sequence is reachable
- [ ] bytes a live process's rows cover are never returned to the pool
- [ ] the spec sentence for the rule

**Lead review, SO-91.p1: a split does not free bytes a live process's rows
cover.**

**The finding, reproduced on the unfixed kernel on all three profiles:**
root creates a child that holds a pattern and parks; root splits a piece
off the child's destination and releases it; the bytes go back to the
pool; root cuts them again as a fresh `Memory`, maps it, finds the
child's pattern and overwrites a word; the child wakes and sees its data
changed. Two neighbours were reachable too (probed on riscv32): a mapped
region split and released while mapped, after which a fresh piece shares
its bytes; and **a piece cut off a child's destination accepted by
`slab_donate`, which puts kernel slabs inside memory the child's image
occupies.**

**The rule: a split carries a region's rows to whichever piece covers
them.** `MemoryOp.Split` finds where the cut will fall (`pool_cut_base`),
refuses a cut through the middle of any row (`rows_clear_of_cut`), and
after the new slot is written moves each row lying in it, with its count,
to that slot (`rows_move_to_piece`). Attribution is by the row's region
and its physical range. A `Memory` with no rows counted on it takes the
old path. Everything that drops a row's reference (unmap, teardown) drops
it on the slot the row now names, so none of those changed.
- **Why not "a `Memory` with rows cannot be split":** launchers split
  regions with rows on them today, legitimately (the hole piece cut from a
  child's destination; a mapped region), and every existing case keeps
  its meaning under this rule.
- **The refused cut answers `NoResource`,** a status, with nothing
  changed: a launcher cannot know where a child's segments lie, the
  reasoning the spec gives for `map` over an image row.
- **Donation needed no change of its own:** `slab_donate` already refuses
  a region with rows counted on it, and a piece that covers rows now
  carries them.
- Spec §2.5 states the rule.

**Cases, all three profiles:**
- `split_live_bytes`: while the child lives the fresh piece does not
  contain its pattern and the child finds it intact; after the child's
  death the same request returns its bytes.
- `split_mapped_bytes`: a cut through a mapped row is refused; a fresh
  piece does not share a mapped piece's bytes; after the unmap it does.
- `split_rows_held`: cuts through an image row and through the stack's
  row are refused; a front piece covering rows is a `BadState` fault at
  donation.
- With the rows not moved, all three fail on all three profiles; with the
  mid-row refusal off, the two that cut mid-row fail.

**Runs:** the three cases 10 of 10 on each profile; the full suite (667:
nine new rows and no other row moved); the C3 smoke; the S3 smoke. The
runner's diff removes no line.

**Not exercised:**
- A row that names the IMAGE's `Memory` (segments used in place, SO-84.p2,
  in review): the attribution does not care whose region a row names, but
  no case shows a split of an image with in-place rows on it. The lead
  adds that trial when both have merged.
- The neighbour probes ran unfixed on riscv32 only.
- A parked call's body in a split region directly; it is the same hazard
  and the rows now hold the bytes.

**With SO-84.p2 and SO-90.p1** (both in review): all three touch
`kernel/core/process.saw` and `dispatch.saw` in different functions.
Whichever merges later is rebased by the lead.

Approved patch SO-91.p1 (revision 1)

**Approved (rev 1).**

**Read.**
- `pool_cut_base` walks exactly as `pool_cut` does (the first free range that holds the length on a pool root, else the region's own base), so the range the refusal looks at is the range the cut takes.
- `rows_clear_of_cut` and `rows_move_to_piece` go over every process body and match on the row's `region`. A device row and the stats row carry `NO_MEMORY` (the loader's device grant, `IoMemoryOp.Map`'s `install_row`, the stats map), so neither is ever taken for a row of the region being cut.
- A moved row lowers the parent's count and raises the piece's, and `remove_grant` drops its reference on the row's own `region`, so the unmap and the teardown follow the row to its piece with no change of their own.

**Run on origin/main + rev 1, under v0.23.0:** `split_live_bytes`, `split_mapped_bytes` and `split_rows_held`, 5 of 5 on riscv32, riscv32-flat and arm64. One-line mutants, once per profile, all killed on all three:

| mutant | first case to fail |

| the rows not moved to the piece | `split_live_bytes`: "while the child lives, witness found=0" is missing |
