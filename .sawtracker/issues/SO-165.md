**Interrupt-driven UART transmit, and a real ESP32-S3 UART driver.**

Today every console driver is one thread with one loop.
- **The emulators' UART drivers** (`svc-uart-ns16550`, `svc-uart-pl011`) read through interrupts, but write synchronously inside the request.
- **The S3's console** is `svc-console`, which makes one kernel `debug_print` per byte. When UART0's FIFO is full, that print stalls with interrupts masked. That stall is the console share measured in the flash-speed runs.

**The owner's direction (Nov 25):**
- **Serving.** A serving thread at a high band takes a `Write`, copies its bytes into the driver's own ring, and answers once they are queued.
- **Transmit is interrupt-driven.** The driver fills the hardware FIFO, then sleeps until the TX-empty interrupt and tops it up. Nothing spins, and no background thread is needed.
- **A full ring holds the `Write`** (the protocol already allows a held request), so the writer slows down and nothing is lost.
- **The kernel's own console** (boot lines, panics) stays synchronous.
- **Bands.** Once this exists, the console driver declares `bands = "full"` and serves at High or Rt0.

**Scope:**
- A new S3 UART driver for UART0, with its interrupt line, as the console's server on the S3.
- The same transmit path in the ns16550 and pl011 drivers.
- Cases for back-pressure, for the hold when the ring is full, and for no lost bytes.
- A board run of the stress profile with the new driver.

It needs a design first; I will brief one.

<!-- sawtracker:comment {"author":"agent:claude-sawos","body_bytes":366,"created":"1795731310","id":"c5"} -->
**Owner ruling (Nov 26):** the kernel learns that a userspace driver owns UART0 through an **explicit claim**. It's an op on the UART0 `IoMemory` handle behind its own right, made once the driver's interrupt path runs. A death or a release ends it, and a panic overrides it. The kernel does not infer ownership from the driver mapping the window or binding the line.

<!-- sawtracker:comment {"author":"agent:claude-sawos","body_bytes":428,"created":"1795731388","id":"c6"} -->
**Owner refinement (Nov 26):** the claim is a **generic device claim** on any `IoMemory` handle, not a UART-specific op. Proposed semantics, for the design to settle:
- on a device the kernel itself uses (`kernel = true`, today the console), the kernel hands it over while the claim holds, and a panic overrides it for the console;
- on any other device, the claim is exclusive ownership: an overlapping second claim is refused.

**Notes for SO-165.p1 (U1) and SO-165.p2 (U3).**

**p1, `uartproto` (U1):**
- **New ops and answers:**
  - `Stats` is op 6, after `Pending` (5). It answers `Ok` plus eight u32 counters (received, lost, frame, parity, breaks, queued, sent, writes_held), read by `Uart.stats()`.
  - `Lost` (4) and `Garbled` (5) answer a `Read` only. Each carries four u32 counters (lost, frame, parity, break) and decodes to `UartError.Lost`/`Garbled`.
  - A mark on any other op, or of the wrong length, is Malformed.
- **Shared code:** the `Write`/`Sync` wording and the mark rules are in the module doc. The saturating `count_up` moved from consolecore into uartproto.
- **The drivers:**
  - `svc-console` answers `Stats` through `consolecore`.
  - ns16550 and pl011 count received, lost, queued and sent; frame, parity, breaks and writes_held are zero there.
  - `tests/uart-text-client` asserts queued = sent = 364 with no errors, against all three drivers. Its BadOp refusal moved to op 7, and a refusal of `Stats` carrying a byte is added.
- **Runner expectations moved,** all exact counts of deterministic paths:
  - `console_service`: served 71→75, refused 7→8, printed 837→945, traps 967→1081;
  - `console_service_uart`: served 76→80, answered 17→19, discarded 55→57, traps 148→154.
- **Mutants, all red under `blade test`:** the Stats op number; Lost decoded as Garbled; the Stats length check removed; queued and sent swapped; `refusal(Lost)` answering Busy; the console's sent reported as 0.
- **Totals:** `make sos-test` passed, 1596.

**p2, `esp32s3io` UART0 block (U3):**
- **Registers:** offsets, interrupt bits (the receive set `INT_RX` = 0x19D), and the STATUS, CONF1 and MEM_CONF fields, checked against ESP-IDF v5.4.1's `uart_reg.h`. Each word keeps every bit of its register it doesn't own.
- **Threshold checks:** `check_uart_thresholds` adds three `ConfigError` cases.
- **Orders over a new `UartPort` trait:** `start_uart`; `service_rx` (reads INT_ST, drains the count and recounts until 0, then clears exactly the bits seen, and never acknowledges); `fill_tx` (one STATUS read, never reads FIFO); and the transmit interrupt and threshold.
- **`ABI.md` §4b is new.**
- **Mutants, 16, all red:** the offsets, the receive set, CONF1 read-modify-write, RX_TOUT_EN, the shifts, clearing all receive bits, no recount loop, the start without its clear, the fill ignoring room or cap, transmit-on without its clear, a threshold of 128 and a timeout of 0.
- **Differences from the design:**
  - `RegisterPort` is write-only, so a new `UartPort` adds `read_word(&var self)`.
  - UART0 has its own `UartRegister` type rather than a `Block` variant, which would have added arms to the GPIO and PWM drivers' exhaustive matches.
  - The counters are u32.
  - The receive clear takes exactly the bits INT_ST showed, so an error after the read still fires.
- **Totals:** `make sos-smoke-esp32s3` passed, 519, 6 board-only not run.

Approved patch SO-165.p1 (revision 1)

**Approved.** U1 of SO:s3-uart 3.2 (r6). I checked it on main with sawlang v0.24.6.

**The wire matches the design:**
- `Lost = 4` and `Garbled = 5`, each carrying four little-endian `u32` counts: lost, frame, parity, break.
- `Stats` answers `Ok` and eight `u32`s in the design's order: received, lost, frame, parity, breaks, queued, sent, writes held (`STATS_BYTES = 32`, words at 1 to 29).
- `stats_reply` refuses any other length.
- **Op 6, not the design's 5:** `Stats` is op 6 because SO-172 took 5 for `Pending` after the design was written. The design's text and the spec unit should say 6.

**Two mutants of my own, both red:**
- The NS16550 driver reporting `sent: 0` fails `console_service_uart` ("stats: every byte queued and sent, none lost"), and `console_service`, which doesn't use that driver, stays green.
- `stats_reply` reading `lost` from word 9 fails the hosted `client` test ("a stats answers its counters").

**The drivers.** NS16550 writes each byte to the device as it's queued, so its `sent` equals `queued` by construction. The runner's moved counts follow from `uart-text-client`'s added calls, the `Stats` call and the `Stats` refusal, and are exact on deterministic paths.
