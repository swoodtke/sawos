## Description

**sosrt runs Saw's background tasks: the freestanding executor set and SL-1150's drive and close.** sawlang's SL-1150 (design done, Nov 17) lets `Task.spawn` (and `TaskGroup`) work freestanding for a runtime that claims the thread's background group: two compiler-defined entries, `__saw_background_drive() -> Int64` (one bounded turn over the thread's tasks; -1 nothing live, 0 runnable now, INT64_MAX waiting with no deadline, else the earliest deadline in monotonic ns) and `__saw_background_close()` (cancel, join, release). sosrt exports none of the freestanding executor set today, so any spawn fails at link in a sawos image.

**Build (after SO-124 gives threads a real entry, and after the sawlang release carrying SL-1150):** the executor set sosrt must export (from sawlang's runtime/ABI.md); the per-thread executor word in the architecture's thread register (riscv32's `tp` and the S3's `THREADPTR` are already saved per thread in the trap frame; arm64's `TPIDR_EL0` is not and must join the frame, a kernel change); the drive in `sos`'s blocking wrappers (`Waiter.wait` and the pipe calls), bounding the wait by the drive's deadline with a Timer attached to the Waiter and ending it from another thread with an Event (`reactor_wake`); the close on every thread's return into sosrt's thread entry; the declaration on the process side only (the kernel image keeps `Task.spawn` refused). `Process.exit` does not close (the teardown frees the whole process).

**SO-141.p1 (S0, spec text) note.**
**Numbers:** WAIT_WORD_OP 0xFFF8, THREAD_WAKE_OP 0xFFF7 and PARK_OP 0xFFF6, below LOCK_WAKE_OP; OP_BAND_FLOOR becomes PARK_OP. The statuses are TimedOut 12 and Woken 13. The child Handle default is 16.
**Sections:** §2.1 (Call bounded by the wait word, the early-end table, the full-table rule, and the parked-claim rules), §2.2 (Wait's deadline and wake), §2.6, §2.9, §3's band sentence, §5.7 (statuses and the no-handle band table), §7 (the wait word and its values, the order at a park, the wake and its latch, same-process only, nothing assuming one core), §8 (the close at a thread's end, Join's early end, the latch reset), §10.1 (the task executor; HandlerGroup moves to §10.2), and §11 (the thread gate, not built).
**Lead rulings folded in:**
- A claim held by a wakeable park can't be attached, given, or transferred by any route: the BadState fault.
- A wakeable Resolve detaches an attached claim first.
- PeerClosed spends the claim.
- **Time stays a capability:** WAIT_WORD_OP(addr, clock) takes a Monotonic Clock with Read, or none. A thread with none may write only 0 or UInt64.max, and a finite deadline faults AccessDenied at the park. The check is made once, at registration, so a bounded park stays one trap. The runtime registers lazily, re-registering with the Clock before a thread's first finite deadline.
- A refused ClockGet makes the executor panic.
**For K1:** drivers/svc-pwm-esp32s3/process.toml declares handles = 15, below the new default of 16.

Requested changes on patch SO-141.p1 (revision 1)

**Held for a revision.** The new text follows design 2.1 and your rulings almost everywhere. The problems are where two rules meet, and in older text the patch left stale. The spec binds K1, K2 and R1–R3, so these are worth fixing before they're built against.

An agent read the new text against the design and against today's code; it listed what agrees. I rechecked the items marked ✓ myself, and the rest carry its line references (spec.md at HEAD).

**To fix:**
1. **✓ The reply park begun at the room park's resume.** The text reads the word "each time an op of the thread's would park", naming `Call`'s room park and reply park (4529–4534), "once per park" (4557–4559), and "only by its own thread's trap" (4619–4620). But a wakeable `Call` that room-parked stages and parks on its claim "at the room park's resume" (396–404). In the code, `resume_call_room(t, c)` runs for the parked caller `t` inside the trap of the thread that freed the room (refs.saw:1907, 1948), so that reply park can't re-read `t`'s word. Say which holds; it binds K2. The likely answer is that the deadline recorded at the room park covers the reply park that follows.
2. **The claim-transfer ruling (425–431).**
   - (a) "Nor can the handle leave the table by any route while the park holds it" contradicts 422–424, which let another thread release it.
   - (b) At a room-park resume, a sibling's record rewritten to carry the parked claim is re-validated where no fault can be raised. Say whether it "arrives empty" (797–800, as `hold_staged` leaves a failing slot) or is released (`release_carried`), and add the check to those questions.
   - (c) The new `BadState` isn't placed in the ordered answers it joins: `ProcessOp.Give` (3336–3341), `Waiter.give` (3345–3352), the carried-handle faults (3368–3377) and §2.2's attach rule (928–932).
3. **✓ §10.1 says "every finite deadline the executor writes comes from a clock reading" (5256–5259), but the wrapper writes `1` when the drive answers 0 (5352–5354).** `1` is a finite deadline, so a thread registered with no Clock faults `AccessDenied` at a park that isn't ready. The re-registration rule has to cover the `1` path, and the premise sentence should say so, or an implementer will re-register only where clock readings are made.
4. **✓ Stale text says `Resolve` always consumes:**
   - "`Resolve` parks and consumes on every path … ends the exchange whichever way it returns" (310);
   - "destroy the caller's handle entry" (246–248, 269–270).

   These now contradict the wakeable `Resolve` that borrows (411–416). The qualifier at 319–320 reads as if it were about `Ready`.
5. **✓ `WAIT_WORD_OP` with address 0:** "unregisters it, whatever the second argument" (4570–4572) contradicts the ordered answers, which decide the `clock` word first (4573–4577). Say which comes first.
6. **✓ `PARK_OP` and the latch:**
   - `PARK_OP` is "always wakeable" (4588–4592), but step 2 checks the latch only "on a wakeable word", and a word of 0, or none, is plain (4538, 4542). Say that `PARK_OP` reads the latch whatever its word.
   - "No caller that never writes one sees a new status" (3756) is untrue of `PARK_OP`.

**To settle (the agent's, plausible):**
7. **The quota row:**
   - After an early end, a bounded `send` holds its claim while it drives, and a task in that drive can make its own bounded `send`. So the rows grow with send-nesting depth, not "one per thread" (2769–2771, 5406–5409).
   - The fallback keeps that working, so only the statement is off.
8. **The SMP bullet (4617–4624):**
   - It drops the design's premise, "under that lock": two kernel sections can't interleave on several cores only because of SMP's one lock.
   - "As every cross-core wake does" names a mechanism this spec doesn't define, where 4650–4652 still says uniprocessor.
   - The per-core Timers sentence is gone.
9. **"A refused `ClockGet` panics" (5285–5287).** A missing right or System handle is an `AccessDenied` or `BadHandle` fault, not a status the runtime can panic on. Name the read-first step, as `rt_write`'s `may_print` reads rights.
10. **An early end after another thread released the claim:** "the claim is still the caller's under the same word" (414–416) leaves the parked thread's own reference, and what a later `Resolve` on that word answers, unstated.
11. **The one-word lock's no-block threads** (from r1):
    - a no-block waiter "yields and swaps again" (4511–4513), but such a thread can't yield (4381–4382);
    - re-acquiring a held word is `BadState` (4519–4520) only for a thread with a block.

**Cleanups:**
- **Stale text:**
  - Timer is still "the process-sleep primitive" (1835–1836, 45);
  - "One hardware timer, two customers" (1889) gains "a third customer" (1905);
  - §10.2's `send` "suspends in place" and its "reactor Waiter" (5428–5431, 5483) contradict §10.1.
- **"What is built" and §11:** §8's "What is built" (4752–4762) lists ops that aren't built yet, and §11 lists only the thread gate as unbuilt. Either mark them pending until K1 lands, or say the text is ahead of the code.
- **Nits:**
  - `ThreadBlock` is §5c, not §7 (5249);
  - say whether a cross-process wake writes `LOCK_WAIT`'s console line before `BadArg` (4596–4603);
  - `reactor_poll`'s `timeout_ms × 10^6` needs the saturation `sleep_ns` has (5320).

**On the Clock ruling:** as the kernel text stands, it leaks no time. A Clock-less thread can write only 0 or `NEVER_NS`, a finite word faults before any clock comparison, and `PARK_OP` with no word, 0 or `NEVER_NS` has no deadline. Item 3 is the executor's side of the same rule. It's a ruling beyond the approved design, so the owner should see it.
