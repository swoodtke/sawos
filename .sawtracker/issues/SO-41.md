## The contract (spec §2.7, three groups; read the passage in full)

1. **Creation ops** (`ThreadCreate`, `EventCreate`, `WaiterCreate`,
   `PipeCreate`) act on behalf of the process the handle names, through any
   Process handle carrying the op's right.
   - **That process owns the new object and is charged for it.** A thread
     created through a child's handle runs in the child, against the child's
     Thread quota.
   - **The new handle(s) go in the caller's table**, charged to the caller's
     `Handle` row.
   - **A caller error faults the caller:** a bad entry or stack (`BadEntry`), a
     bad mode (`BadArg`).
   - **Through a `Gone` process's handle these answer `Revoked`** (§2.10),
     after the rights and argument checks.
     - A `Gone` process has no body, so `ThreadCreate`'s entry and stack check
       can't run against its address space.
     - Decide the order: `Revoked` before the address-space check, as the
       argument check that needs a body. Document it at the site and in the
       spec.
2. **`Exit`, `ThreadSelf` and `BootHandleNext` are self-only.** Through a
   handle naming another process, a `Gone` one included, they fault the caller
   with `NotSelf`. `Exit` already does; make the other two match.
3. **`Start`, `Give`, `GetStatus` and `Stats`** act on the named process, as
   today. **`Start` and `Give` on a non-`Created` child stay `BadState`.**

`InterruptBind` is no longer a Process op: SO-63 moves it to System. If SO-63
has merged, ignore it. If it hasn't, leave `ProcessOp.InterruptBind` exactly as
it is and say so.

## The change

1. **The creation ops**
   (`kernel/core/dispatch.saw` `thread_create`, `event_create`,
   `waiter_create`, `pipe_create`, and their `process_op_decoded` arms):
   - mint the handle(s) into the **caller's** table (`p`);
   - set the object's owner and charge its quota to the **named** process
     (`obj.slot`);
   - fault `p` on every caller error.
   
   `pipe_create` today mints both ends into the owner's table and copies them
   out to the caller; change it to mint into the caller's. Check the quota
   pre-flights: the object row against the named process, the `Handle` row and
   table room against the caller.
2. **The strand goes away.** The comments about "the teardown assumes the dying
   process is the caller … never scheduled again" (sched.saw `CURRENT_THREAD`,
   §2.7, §5.7, §11) become false once no creation op faults the named process.
   Remove them, and confirm no remaining path ends another process while a
   caller runs. If one remains, ask.
3. **Self-only:** `ThreadSelf` and `BootHandleNext` fault `NotSelf` when
   `obj.slot != p`. Update the comment above `BootHandleNext` that states the
   unchecked identity.
4. **Typed tier** (`kernel/sysapi/src/system.saw`):
   - the creation methods' docs say whose table the handle lands in and who is
     charged;
   - the wrappers they return are now correct for a cross-process create, since
     the word is in the caller's table;
   - check the C floor docs too.
5. **Spec:** remove the §2.7 markers (the creation-op and self-only ones), the
   §2.10 not-yet item about creation ops through a `Gone` handle, §5.7's fault
   marker, §11's line, and §12's "creation ops place handles in the caller's
   table; not yet enforced". Edit mixed markers down to what remains.

## Tests (probe first)

Model them on the `process_*`, `thread_*` and `give_*` cases. Each asserts its
observable.
1. **Cross-process create.** Root creates, through a child's Process handle, a
   Thread (in the child), an Event, a Waiter and a pipe.
   - Each handle works in root's table: join the thread; signal and wait the
     Event; use the Waiter; post and take on the pipe.
   - The child's quota is charged, and root's `Handle` row is: show it by
     exhausting the child's Thread quota through root's creates.
   - Root can give each on to the child.
2. **Caller errors fault the caller.** A bad entry in a cross-process
   `ThreadCreate` faults root's helper process, not the child: the child lives
   on and its status is unchanged. The same for a bad `EventCreate` mode. Use
   a helper process as the faulting caller so root survives to assert.
3. **Through a `Gone` handle,** each creation op answers `Revoked`.
4. **Self-only:** `ThreadSelf` and `BootHandleNext` through a child's handle
   fault the caller `NotSelf`.
5. **No strand:** after test 2's fault, the caller's own process is torn down
   normally (its teardown line appears), and nothing is left hung.

Completing the target-vs-caller sweep (claude-air-reviewer, SO-26 c12). `BootHandleNext` and `ThreadSelf` check their right on the handle used but act on the **caller** (`boot_handle_next(p, …)`, `CURRENT_THREAD`). **Lead review, SO-41.p1: which process a Process op acts on (spec §2.7).** This also closes SO-44.

**Creation ops.** `thread_create`, `event_create`, `waiter_create` and `pipe_create` take `(p, owner, ...)`.
- The object's `process` and its charge are the named process (`owner`).
- Handles mint into the caller's table: the `Handle` row and table room are checked in `mint_handle(p)`.
- Caller errors fault `p`: `BadEntry`, and `BadArg` for a bad mode.
- The thread's entry and stack are checked against the owner's body.

**`Gone` owner.** It answers `Revoked` after the rights and argument checks and before the quota, via `has_body`. `ThreadCreate` answers before its entry/stack check, which needs a body. This is documented in §2.7.

**Self-only.** `ThreadSelf` and `BootHandleNext` fault `NotSelf` when `obj.slot != p`.

**Strand.** No path ends another process while a caller runs. The `CURRENT_THREAD` and `end_process` comments are rewritten; `has_body`'s skip stays as a defence.

**Probe (old kernel).**
- A cross-process `ThreadCreate` handed root a host-relative word.
- A bad entry faulted the host and stranded the helper.
- `ThreadSelf` and `BootHandleNext` didn't fault.
- Creation through a `Gone` handle answered `QuotaExceeded`.

**Tests.**
- `create_cross`: four cross-process creates used from root's table, the host's Thread quota charged, all given on, and `Revoked` after the host ends.
- `create_cross_fault`: four helpers fault with `BadEntry`, `BadArg` and `NotSelf` ×2 while the host stays Running, each helper torn down normally.
- Mutants caught: minting into the owner's table, faulting the owner, and dropping either `NotSelf` check.

**Spec.** The markers are removed from §2.7, §2.10, §5.7, §11 and §12.

Requested changes on patch SO-41.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SO-41.p1, changes requested: a §8 contract break that needs a user ruling, a wrong `pipe_create` refusal, and test gaps. The mechanics are sound.**

**What checks out** (by reading 72d231f + p1):
- **Every creation op is atomic.** Nothing is written before the mint into the caller succeeds, and `pipe_create`'s second-mint failure unbinds the inlet from the CALLER's table.
- **`has_body(owner)` precedes every body read.** ThreadCreate's region check is the only one, and it comes after. A Created owner has a body, and nothing preempts between the check and the use.
- **Every `fault_process`/`end_process` site targets the caller** (about 180 in dispatch.saw, all `p`/`caller`). The sched.saw sites are the current thread's process, so the rewritten no-strand comment holds.
- **The `NotSelf` checks come before any side effect.** Cross-process threads run in the owner, are charged to and credited back to it (or nobody once disowned), and a cross-process `Join` answers `Revoked` after the owner's death.

**1. A thread can now run in a Created (unstarted) child. This breaks spec §8 and needs a user ruling.**
- `thread_create` checks only `has_body(owner)` (dispatch.saw ~1559), and `Thread.Start` checks only the thread's own state (~1620-1632). `run_thread` then loads the owner's domain, which exists from `process_create`.
- Now that the Thread handle lands in the launcher's table, a launcher can `ThreadCreate` through its Created child's handle and `Start` that thread BEFORE `ProcessOp.Start`.
- That breaks §8's "Two phases, inert until started … the start call is the barrier that makes a half-populated table unrepresentable" (spec ~2647-2657). The early thread can drain the boot set while gives are still arriving, and `start(boot_tag)` can then deliver a word twice.
- It also re-opens "a Gone child was always started" (spec ~1121-1123, the §2.10 Process row). The patch removed the "a Created child can die unstarted" marker, but a launcher-started thread that exits or faults as the last live thread runs `end_process` on a child still `Created`.
- Two side effects:
  - launcher-created threads use up the child's Thread row, so the child's own `Start` can answer `QuotaExceeded`;
  - a never-started thread created by a third party counts as live, and the owner can't even see its handle to start or release it.
- The options:
  - (a) `Thread.Start` faults `BadState` while the owner is `Created`: creating is harmless, starting is what breaks the barrier;
  - (b) refuse `ThreadCreate` into a `Created` owner;
  - (c) rewrite §8/§2.7 to allow it.

**2. `pipe_create` answers `NoResource` where its docs say `QuotaExceeded`.** This is a pre-existing code defect the new docs contradict.
- With exactly one Handle row left, the inlet mint takes it, and the outlet mint fails on quota. The unwind's `unbind_handle` credits the row back, so `mint_refusal(caller)` re-asks, finds room, and answers `NoResource` (dispatch.saw ~3114-3122).
- system.saw ~1141-1147 ("a caller without room for two more handles, is `SosStatus.QuotaExceeded`") and §2.9's policy-first rule say otherwise. It's reachable by any child: the handle quota is 12 and the table holds 16.
- Fix: pre-flight `quota_room_for(caller, Handle, 2)` and table room before `alloc_pipe`, or take the status before the unbind.

**3. Tests that would miss a regression** (predicted by reading):
- Event/Waiter/Pipe ownership and the owner's charge are unpinned; only Thread's is, by the 5th-create refusal. Charging the caller, or setting `process` to the caller, survives.
- No refusal-rollback case for any op. The pipe unwind deleted (a leaked inlet naming a Free slot) or done in the owner's table (the old code: a `fatal_kernel` or an owner entry unbound) survives.
- `Gone` plus a bad argument is unpinned: a bad mode, pipe's bad cap or buffer. `has_body` moved before them survives.
- The stack-only `BadEntry` arm: role 1's entry arm fires first.
- The caller's Handle-row charge is unshown (root is unlimited), though the brief asked for it.

**4. Stale docs:**
- ops.saw ~257-262 ("through another process's handle the word that comes back is that process's"), ~268-272, ~307-311 ("the table of the process the handle names");
- rights.saw ~233-234 and ~269-270;
- dispatch.saw ~1151-1155 (`process_start` as "the one site where the slot is written for somebody else"), ~3082 ("both tables");
- system.saw ~703 (a Waiter has no mode);
- spec §2.10 ~1482-1486 states "a malformed argument faults exactly as … on a live object" universally, which ThreadCreate's Revoked-before-entry/stack exception (~1109-1111) contradicts. Add a cross-reference.

**5. Nit, pre-existing:** all four ops ask the caller's Handle quota AFTER the owner's slab, so a caller at its budget facing a full slab gets `NoResource`, against policy-first.
