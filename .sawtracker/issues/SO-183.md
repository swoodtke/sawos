# Networking A5c: TCP congestion control, fast retransmit and fast recovery

The networking design is `SO:networking`, §6.1 and §10's track A. A5b
("netstack: TCP data transfer, retransmission and the stack hook") is
approved and merging. It gives `drivers/netstack/src/tcpconn.saw` data
transfer, RFC 6298 retransmission with Karn and backoff, and the Stack hook,
but **no congestion control**: the peer's window is the only limit on what
is in flight. Read SO-182's notes (the implementer's note and r3 note) and
the reviewer's verdicts there, for the shape of the code and what was
already found and fixed.

## Build (hosted, in `drivers/netstack`)

RFC 5681, with RFC 6582 (NewReno) for recovery:
1. **The congestion window and slow-start threshold:**
   - `cwnd`, with the initial window of RFC 5681 §3.1 (or RFC 6928's, if
     you choose it; say which);
   - `ssthresh` at its arbitrarily high start;
   - what is sent is limited by min(cwnd, rwnd).
2. **Slow start and congestion avoidance:**
   - Grow `cwnd` by bytes acked, with RFC 3465's appropriate byte counting
     at L = 2·SMSS in slow start, or say why not.
   - In avoidance, grow by about SMSS per RTT.
3. **On an RTO** (RFC 5681 §3.1, eq. 4): `ssthresh` = max(FlightSize/2,
   2·SMSS), and `cwnd` = 1 SMSS (the loss window).
4. **Fast retransmit and fast recovery** (RFC 5681 §3.2, with RFC 6582's
   NewReno partial-ACK rule):
   - the third duplicate ACK retransmits the first unacked segment;
   - `ssthresh` and the inflated window;
   - each further duplicate ACK inflates the window;
   - a partial ACK retransmits the next hole and stays in recovery;
   - a full ACK deflates and leaves recovery;
   - define "duplicate ACK" exactly as RFC 5681 §2 does.
5. **Restart after idle** (RFC 5681 §4.1).
6. **Limited transmit** (RFC 3042) is optional. Say whether you did it.
7. **The state's bounds:** all arithmetic in `UInt64` or saturating, with a
   peer that can make FlightSize and the ACK stream hostile (a forged
   duplicate-ACK storm must not grow `cwnd` past what RFC 5681 §3.2 allows).
   RFC 5961-style ACK validation is A5d's; say what you rely on.

**Out of scope (A5d):** the out-of-order queue, SACK, RFC 5961, keepalive,
zero-window probes and the TIME-WAIT details. Out-of-order data stays
dropped with a duplicate ACK, which is exactly what drives fast retransmit
here.

**The implementer's note for SO-183.p1 (A5c: TCP congestion control, fast retransmit and fast recovery).**

**The state (`tcpconn.saw`):** `cwnd`, `ssthresh` and `avoid_acked` (`UInt64`); `dupacks`; `recovering`; `recover` and `recover_held`; `inflation_left`; `partial_taken`; `resend_owed`; `sent_at`. `Status` reports `cwnd`, `ssthresh`, `dupacks` and `recovering`.

**The rules:**
- **The limit on sending:** min(usable rwnd, `cwnd` − (SND.NXT − SND.UNA)). A bare FIN isn't held by `cwnd`.
- **Opening (RFC 5681 §3.1):**
  - the SYN's acknowledgment opens `cwnd` at the initial window, or at one segment if the SYN or SYN,ACK was resent;
  - `ssthresh` starts at `MAX_CWND` (65535).
- **Slow start (RFC 3465):** growth is min(bytes acked, L). L is 2·SMSS, or SMSS for the ACKs up to the one covering what was in flight at the last timeout (§2.3).
- **Avoidance:** bytes are counted, and `cwnd` gains one SMSS per window acknowledged, the excess carrying over.
- **RTO (eq. 4):**
  - `ssthresh` = max(FlightSize/2, 2·SMSS), with FlightSize = SND.MAX − SND.UNA, and `cwnd` = 1·SMSS;
  - recovery ends, and `recover` = SND.MAX is held;
  - a repeated timeout of the same segment recomputes the same `ssthresh`.
- **A duplicate ACK (§2, exactly):** data in flight, no data, no FIN, ACK == SND.UNA, and the same window.
- **Fast retransmit and recovery (§3.2):**
  - the third duplicate sets `ssthresh` by eq. 4 and `cwnd` = `ssthresh` + 3·SMSS, sets `recover` = SND.MAX, and owes the segment at SND.UNA;
  - the resend goes inside the peer's window, ignores `cwnd`, leaves SND.NXT alone and drops Karn's sample;
  - later duplicates inflate by SMSS while inflations are left;
  - a duplicate during an RTO's hold starts nothing (RFC 6582 step 1).
- **A partial ACK (RFC 6582 step 5):** `cwnd` −= acked (saturating), plus SMSS if acked ≥ SMSS. The next hole is resent, and only the first partial ACK restarts the timer.
- **A full ACK (step 3, option 1):** `cwnd` = min(`ssthresh`, max(FlightSize, SMSS) + SMSS).
- **Restart after idle (§4.1):** after more than an RTO with nothing sent, `cwnd` = min(IW, `cwnd`).

**The choices:**
- the initial window is RFC 5681's, not RFC 6928's: 4·SMSS at or below 1095 bytes and 3·SMSS above, with a `static_assert` holding `OFFERED_MSS` ≤ 2190;
- byte counting per RFC 3465, L = 2·SMSS;
- no limited transmit.

**The bounds against a hostile peer:**
- `cwnd` is clamped to [SMSS, `MAX_CWND`];
- inflation in one recovery is capped at max(3, the segments in flight at entry), so a forged duplicate storm can't pass it;
- FlightSize is bounded by `SEND_BUFFER` plus the FIN;
- `dupacks` saturates.

It relies on A5b's checks: an ACK past SND.MAX is refused, an ACK below SND.UNA is old, and the receive-window test applies. RFC 5961 is A5d's.

Goldens come from an independent Python script, checked against the existing goldens first.

**Gate case `net_tcp_drop`** (riscv32, flat, arm64):
- **The peer:** its `tcp_drop` script offers MSS 256, drops the 5th data segment, ACKs each later segment as a duplicate, and logs the resend only if it came after three or more duplicates.
- **The client:** it writes 4096 bytes and never polls, so only fast retransmit can recover the loss.
- **What's asserted:** the data arrives whole, and the resend came after three duplicates, never when.
- 18 of 18 peer mutants are red.

**A finding for the reviewer and owner:** with `SEND_BUFFER` 2048 and an MSS of 1460, at most two segments are in flight, so fast retransmit can never fire against a full-size peer. It fires only when the peer offers 512 or less, which is why the gate's peer offers 256. Raising `SEND_BUFFER` costs per-connection memory, 8 slots × the increase, and is a separate decision.
