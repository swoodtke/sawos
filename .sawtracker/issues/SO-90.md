## Description

## Finding (from SO-85's implementer, Nov 13)

Reasoned from the code; not reproduced.

A process may be able to stop the machine through the syscall path.

The early copy doors check a buffer when a call is made. A call that parks
keeps the buffer's address, and the late door resolves it again at the
wake. If the row that covered the buffer has gone in between, the late door
refuses, and the kernel treats that refusal as its own bug: `fatal`, exit
status 5.

## The sequence believed to reach it

1. A thread parks with a buffer in a mapped row that lies inside its
   process's copy window: `Waiter.Wait` with its wait record, or
   `pipe_call` on a full ring with its args record and body.
2. Another thread of the same process unmaps that row (`Mapping.unmap`).
3. At the wake the late door runs: `deliver_wait_record`
   (`kernel/core/refs.saw`, "wait record refused by the copy door"), or
   `resume_call_room` ("parked call record/body refused"), or the reply
   path beside them. Each ends in `fatal`.

## What it needs

A mapped row inside the copy window. A mapping outside a process's own
region is no buffer at all, so the row must lie in the hole between the
image's data and the stack, which needs a piece of the process's own
destination `Memory` mapped back into it. Image rows cannot be unmapped,
and since SO-85 a mapping cannot cover one.

Before SO-85 `translate_user` could not fail, so the same sequence read or
wrote through a nonsense address.

## To settle

- A probe: can the sequence be built, on which profiles.
- What the late door should do: end the process whose buffer went (it is
  that process's own doing), not the machine.

## Acceptance

- [ ] the probe, as a case if the sequence is reachable
- [ ] no process can reach a kernel `fatal` through a late copy door

**Lead, Nov 13: reachable, at all six late doors, on riscv32 and
riscv32-flat.** The implementer built the sequence against the unfixed
kernel:

- Root creates a child in a destination it cuts from its RAM, splits off
  a piece that lies in the hole between the child's data and its stack,
  and maps that piece into the child. On the identity profiles the mapping
  is at the memory's own address, inside both of the child's copy windows.
- The child parks with a buffer in that row; root (or the child's own
  second thread) unmaps it; the wake comes.
- The kernel stops: `fault wait record refused by the copy door`, and the
  same at the fused call's reply, the parked call's record (with the
  server end open and closed) and its body.
- arm64 is not affected: a mapping there is placed above the region, so
  it is outside both windows and the child is ended at the call.

So a launcher and its child, or a child alone if it is given the handles,
could stop the machine through the syscall path on the identity profiles
(the ESP32-S3 and the ESP32-C3 are identity profiles too; neither smoke
has such a case).

**The fix, being finished:** a late door asks the early door's check again
before it spends anything; on a refusal it records the fault on the
process (`doom_process`) and disposes of what it was delivering as for a
receiver that died, and the scheduler ends the process with `BadBuffer`.
The death cannot happen inside the delivery, which is walking the tables a
teardown would change. The idle loop moved into the scheduler so that a
death owed by an idle-time delivery is paid before the deadlock test. One
case, seven trials, one per door and context, each with its mutant.

**One change the lead asked for before upload:** dooming a process must
request a reschedule, so that no instruction of a condemned process runs
after the trap in which it was condemned; as first built, its running
thread carried on until it next blocked.

**To file from this:** `Memory.split` on a child's destination has no
guard while rows reference it (the implementer's reading; not tried).

**Lead review, SO-90.p1: a late copy door that finds its buffer gone ends
the process, not the machine.**

**The finding, reproduced on the unfixed kernel** (comment above): on
riscv32 and riscv32-flat, a piece of a child's own destination mapped into
the hole between its data and its stack is a legal buffer; unmapped while
the child is parked, it stopped the machine at each of six late doors.
arm64 is not affected.

**The fix:**
- Each late door asks the early door's check again before it spends
  anything (`wake_one_waiter`, `wake_call_reply`, `resume_call_room` in
  `kernel/core/refs.saw`). On a refusal it records the fault on the
  process (`doom_process`; `ProcessRecord.doom`, `DOOM_PENDING`) and
  disposes of what it was delivering as for a receiver that died: the
  Event keeps its bits; the reply is consumed, its claim dropped and the
  ring slot returned; a parked call's pending state is cleared and the
  next waiter for room is served.
- The scheduler pays the death: `drain_deaths` is the first statement of
  `pick_next`, which ends every trap, and runs in `end_process` and in
  each turn of the idle loop, which moved into `sched.saw` so that a death
  owed by an idle-time delivery is paid before the deadlock test. A doomed
  process that owns the current thread goes through `fault_process`; one
  that does not is torn down as an unstarted one is. Root doomed stops the
  machine, as §8 says of process 0.
- The `fatal`s that remain beside a copy door each follow a check made in
  the same step, with no user code between.
- Spec: §2.2 (a buffer valid when a call parked and gone when the answer
  arrives ends its process as a bad buffer does), §2.1, and §8 (no
  instruction of the process runs after the trap in which the fault was
  recorded, since every return to user mode passes a switch point).

**The lead asked for a reschedule request on dooming and was wrong to:**
the drain is at the head of `pick_next` and every trap ends there, so a
condemned process cannot reach user mode. The implementer showed it
instead: in trial 6 the thread whose own system call dooms its process
calls `exit(96)` next, and the child's status is `BadBuffer`; with the
drain made to skip a process that owns the current thread, the trial
fails.

**The case** `late_door_buffer_gone` (riscv32, riscv32-flat), seven
trials, one child each: the wait record; the fused call's reply; a parked
call's record, its body, and its record with the server end closed; the
wait record unmapped by the child's own second thread; the wait record
with the wake from a Timer while the kernel idles. Each asserts
`BadBuffer` and that root carries on, and where something could leak, that
it did not (the signal not spent; the ring whole). A mutant for each door
and each disposal fails at its own line; one mutant passed at first
(`owns_current` forced false) and the trial 6 teardown line was added to
catch it.

**Also in the case, at the reviewer's suggestion: SO-85's any-covering-row
rule, which had no case.** The hole piece mapped read-write only is a
legal buffer; mapped read-write and read-execute, in either order, a send
body there and a record written there are `BadBuffer`. With
`translate_user` restored to skip-and-look-on, the first of those trials
fails (the send goes through).

**Runs,** under the pinned v0.23.0, after the rebase: the case 10 of 10 on
both profiles; the full suite (660: the new case on its two profiles and
nothing else moved); the C3 smoke; the S3 smoke. The runner's diff removes
no line.

**Not exercised:** a door reached from an interrupt's delivery while a
thread of the doomed process was the interrupted one (the syscall and
idle-time contexts are). The ESP32-S3 and the ESP32-C3 are identity
profiles and would have been reachable the same way; neither smoke has
the case.

**Filed from this, not fixed here (SO-91):** `Memory.split` on a child's
destination has no guard while the child's rows reference it.
