**Networking A2: the Saw stack's first layer, hosted.** Unit A2 of the networking design (`SO:networking`, SO-103 §10). The owner ruled on Nov 24 that TCP/IP is written in Saw, UDP first.

A new library package, `drivers/netstack` (or a name the design's §5 suggests), with the stack's first layer as pure functions and state machines over byte buffers, with no `sos` dependency, so `blade test` runs it on the host and `HOSTED_TESTS` lists it:
- **Ethernet II framing.** Parse and build, the MAC filter (ours, broadcast; multicast off), EtherType dispatch.
- **ARP (RFC 826) for IPv4 over Ethernet.**
  - A cache with a fixed capacity and entry ageing, driven by a caller-supplied monotonic time (no clock inside).
  - Request and reply; answering requests for our address; a pending-resolution queue of bounded depth that drops, counted, when full.
  - Gratuitous ARP on address change.
- **IPv4 (RFC 791).**
  - Header parse and build with the checksum.
  - Refuses, counted:
    - a bad version or IHL;
    - a total length past the frame;
    - a bad checksum;
    - fragments (no reassembly in this unit: MF set or a nonzero offset is dropped and counted, and the docs say so);
    - an unknown protocol.
  - TTL on send. A single interface with address, netmask and gateway: next hop is the gateway off-link.
- **ICMP (RFC 792).** Echo reply to an echo request. Destination unreachable (port) for a UDP datagram to a closed port, rate-limited by a caller-supplied time. Other types are counted and dropped.
- **UDP (RFC 768).**
  - Parse and build with the pseudo-header checksum (zero checksum accepted on receive, always computed on send).
  - A bounded port table binding ports to small socket ids; ephemeral ports from 49152–65535.

**Rule 5.2 applies at the frame boundary:** the layer parses from a private copy of the headers, every length is checked against what was received, and a malformed frame is a counted drop, never a panic. The interface the layer gives upward (deliver a datagram to socket id N, send a datagram from id N to addr:port) and downward (a frame to transmit, a frame received) is plain data, so A3's virtio-net driver and A6's sockproto server can drive it.

**Tests.**
- Golden frames for each protocol, built by hand from the RFCs' layouts. No captures from outside the repo.
- Round trips; every refusal by name and counter.
- ARP resolution end to end between two stack instances wired back to back in a test: ping and UDP echo between them, all hosted.
- Every check has a case that goes red when it is removed or inverted; list the mutants.

**Lead: SO-151.p1 is up: `drivers/netstack`, the TCP/IP stack's first layer.** One commit, 0c87c607, on main 95a23817. It adds Ethernet II, ARP, IPv4, ICMP and UDP over byte buffers, with no `sos` dependency, written from RFCs 791, 792, 826, 768 and 1122, with sections cited where a rule isn't obvious. `HOSTED_TESTS` and CLAUDE.md list it.

**API.**
- `Stack(mac:)`.
- `configure(config, sink)`: a changed address sends a gratuitous ARP.
- `receive(frame, now, sink)`.
- `poll(now, sink)`: retries ARP, and drops frames whose next hop never answered.
- `udp_open(port)`: port 0 takes an ephemeral port from 49152.
- `udp_close`.
- `udp_send(id, to, data, now, sink)`: `SendError` is NotConfigured, NoSocket, TooLong, BadAddress, NoRoute, QueueFull or LinkFull.
- `ping`.
- Readers: `drops(Drop)`, `count(Count)`, `counters()`, `resolved`, `pending_frames`, `config()` and `mac()`.

**The `Sink` trait** carries `transmit(frame)` and `deliver(Delivery, data)`, where a `Delivery` is a `Datagram(id, from)` or an `EchoReply`. A sink answering `Full` is a counted drop, never a wait.

**Time and counters.** Time is milliseconds the caller passes; a clock that runs backwards counts as no time passing. `Drop` has 33 named reasons and `Count` has 10.

**Rule 5.2.** `receive` copies the frame, up to 1514 bytes, once into a local buffer, and every parser works on that copy.

**Privacy.** Every field an invariant rests on is `private`, with readers:
- the ARP cache and pending queue;
- the counters, the rate limit, the port table and every `Stack` field;
- `ipv4.Config`'s address, netmask and gateway. Only the validating init builds one, and nothing outside can rewrite it.
- `ipv4.Header`'s `header_bytes` and `total`, and `udp.Header`'s `length`, which bound slices. Only parsing makes a header.

The `//` notes sit above private fields because of the pin's doc-comment bug.

**How the privacy was held.** 56 probe modules were compiled against `src/` with `sawc`:
- all 53 refusals fail to compile, and the 3 controls compile;
- against a copy with every `private ` removed, all 52 access and build probes compile, so `private` does the refusing and not the method-name clash.

The probes are scratch, not a gate case. Once SO-149.p1 has merged, revision 2 will add them to `net_fields_sealed`.

**Tests.** `blade test` passes (3 files), and so does the runner's hosted step.
- `wire.saw`: golden frames for each protocol from the RFC layouts, with checksums worked by hand; RFC 1071's own example; every parser refusal; config validation; and the cache, queue, port table and rate limit at their edges.
- `stack.saw`: through a recording sink, every refusal by name and counter with nothing sent or delivered; replies byte for byte; ARP resolution, retry and expiry; ageing; back-pressure; every `SendError`; and identification wrap.
- `pair.saw`: two stacks back to back resolve each other, then exchange a ping, a UDP echo and a closed-port unreachable, and re-resolve after ageing.

**Mutants.** 127 run, all killed. They cover:
- the checksum and addresses;
- Ethernet;
- the ARP packet, cache and queue;
- every IPv4 check;
- config validation;
- ICMP and UDP parsing;
- the port table;
- the stack's own logic.

**Settled while writing:**
- An echo from 0.0.0.0 is a counted drop (`IcmpNoSource`); before, it would have been answered through the gateway.
- `BadAddress` refuses a send to our own address. There is no loopback, so it would have waited on ARP forever.

**Deferred or unpinned:**
- **The agent's own constants:**
  - an 8-entry ARP cache with a 60 s lifetime;
  - 1 s between requests (RFC 1122 §2.3.2.1);
  - a 3 s pending timeout, with 4 pending frames shared;
  - 1 s between unreachables;
  - 16 sockets and TTL 64.
- **A full pending queue** refuses the new frame, rather than keeping the latest per destination.
- **Not built:** reassembly, protocol-unreachable, multicast/IGMP, loopback, IP options, and refusing class E or 127/8 sources.
- **Received ICMP errors** are counted, not passed to sockets; that falls to the sockets unit.
- **No address yet:** the stack takes no IPv4 until it has an address, so DHCP (A4) must extend that.
- **Memory:** a `Stack` value is about 6.5 KB, and one receive chain holds about 4.5 KB of locals. Size the S3's svc-net stack accordingly.

Requested changes on patch SO-151.p1 (revision 1)

**Changes requested: a reply with no route is counted nowhere and spends the unreachable budget; six mutants survive, one of them a crash path; and smaller points.** The package's tests pass unmodified (3/3). Your note already plans revision 2 for the privacy probes in `net_fields_sealed`, so these can ride in it.

**1. A refused reply is counted nowhere, against "Every refusal is counted by its reason" (lib.saw's module doc).**
- **The echo reply and the port unreachable** both end in `send_ipv4(…) catch { return }`, so `NoRoute`, `QueueFull` and `TxFull` are dropped without a count.
- **`port_unreachable` spends `self.unreachable.allow(now)` before it tries the send**, so a failed unreachable uses up the one-a-second budget.
- **Run** (a first-pass agent's behaviour probe, chat f429 `behave.saw`, which I reran on the clean tree), with no gateway:
  - an echo from 192.0.2.7 gives `frames 0 drops 0 echo replies sent 0`;
  - a UDP datagram from 192.0.2.7 to a closed port is counted only as `udp no port`;
  - UDP from on-link B 500 ms later then gets no unreachable: `icmp rate limited x1`.
- **The fix:** count the send's refusal by its reason, and spend the budget only on a send that goes out (or count a held-back one where its time went).

**2. Six mutants survive**, each rerun by me alone against the package's `blade test`:

| file | the edit | why nothing goes red |
|---|---|---|
| ipv4.saw | `total < header_bytes` → `total < HEADER_BYTES` | every short-total test uses IHL 5 |
| lib.saw | UDP's `datagram[ip.header_bytes()..]` → `[ipv4.HEADER_BYTES..]` | no stack-level test carries IP options |
| lib.saw | ICMP's `data_at = ip.header_bytes() + …` → `ipv4.HEADER_BYTES + …` | the same |
| counters.saw | `total_drops`' saturating sum → a plain add | needs about 2^32 drops |
| lib.saw | the echo reply's `catch { return }` → `catch { }` | `tx_full` is tested only through `udp_send` |
| lib.saw | the ARP send's `catch { return }` → `catch { }` | the same |

The first matters most. With it, a header longer than the stated total reaches the payload slice: the agent's frame (IHL 9, a 36-byte header, total 32, in a 46-byte payload) is refused as `ip total length` on the clean tree, and by its run panics at lib.saw's slice with the comparison loosened. It needs a case with IHL above 5 and a total between 20 and the header's length minus 1, in `wire.saw` and at the stack. The two offset mutants need an echo and a UDP datagram with options at the stack level. The probe shows the code is right there (4 option bytes: echo data `97 98 99 100`, UDP `104 105`, a 32-byte quote that matches). The saturation needs a case or a named equivalent.

**3. Replies go to any source a frame names, through the shared 4-slot pending queue** (run):
- **Our own address:** an echo whose source is our own 10.0.0.1 makes the stack broadcast an ARP request with sender and target both 10.0.0.1. The reply waits in pending, counted `EchoRepliesSent`. `sends_to` refuses our own address for `udp_send` and `ping`, but the reply path doesn't use it.
- **A full queue:** four echoes from unused on-link sources fill the queue, and a local `udp_send` to C then answers `the pending queue is full`.

Refuse our own address (and what `IpBadSource` refuses) as a reply destination. Say whether replies may take the slots local sends need, or give them their own bound.

**4. Smaller points:**
- **`Config`** (ipv4.saw) refuses only `is_broadcast()`, the all-ones address. It accepts the subnet-directed 10.0.0.255/24 and 10.0.0.0/24, a gateway at either, 127.0.0.1 and 240.0.0.1 (run). A stack at 10.0.0.255 then drops a unicast echo to itself as `icmp broadcast echo`, and sends UDP from a source its own `IpBadSource` check drops. Refuse the subnet's broadcast and network addresses, or say why not; 127/8 and class E are on your deferred list.
- **arp.saw's module doc** says a pending frame waits "with its destination address left zero". `send_ipv4` writes the broadcast address before queueing, and the flush overwrites it.
- **A frame queued before `configure` changes the address** goes out with the old source (run). Say whether that is intended.

**5. A question for a ruling: ARP learning.** It creates an entry on any ARP packet to us, as RFC 826 allows. Run:
- an unsolicited reply re-points the gateway;
- a table's worth of unsolicited replies evicts it;
- an off-subnet sender, 255.255.255.255, 224.0.0.1, a zero sender MAC and our own MAC are all learned.

Whether to create only on a request for us or a solicited reply, and to refuse non-unicast sender addresses and those MACs, is the owner's call. The design should record the choice either way.

**What holds** (from the first pass, with my reruns where marked):
- No received frame panics across a sweep of 7,243 frames into two stacks.
- Every packet length is checked before use: IHL at least 5 and within the frame, total between the header and the frame, UDP length from 8 to the payload, ICMP at least 8, ARP at least 28 with its types, lengths and op.
- Checksums: end-around carry, odd length, pseudo-header, a received 0 meaning none, a computed 0 sent as 0xFFFF.
- Fragments are dropped.
- The largest echo is answered whole, with and without options (rerun).
- No reply goes to an IP or link broadcast.
