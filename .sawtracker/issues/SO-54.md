## Description

## Ruled design (user, Nov 9)

`MAX_PROCESSES` is 3 (`kernel/core/limits.saw`). A `Gone` process whose
handles, attachments or charged shared objects are still outstanding keeps a
whole `ProcessSlot`, including its handle table and grant rows, only to keep its
status word and ledger. Split the process into two tables, the same
indirection the lifecycle uses for threads:

- **Process record:** a small entry in its own, larger table. It holds what
  must outlive the process: the generation, the state and status word, the
  quota ledger (used and limits), `refs` (handle entries, attachments and,
  after SO-53, the shared objects charged to it), the attachment, and the final
  trap counters so that `Stats` on a `Gone` process still answers. The pid
  (SO-49) lives here later. Handles name the record, and charged objects
  reference it.
- **Process body:** an entry in the existing `MAX_PROCESSES` table. It holds
  what only a live process needs: the handle table, the protection domain and
  grant rows, and the thread bookkeeping. It is freed at teardown, so a `Gone`
  process never holds one.

A full record table makes `ProcessCreate` answer `NoResource`. Records are
small, so the record table is sized well above the body table; settle the size
in the brief.

## The ruled design (do not reopen)

A `ProcessSlot` today (`kernel/core/pslot.saw` ~187) holds everything about a
process, and a `Gone` process keeps the whole slot (handle table, grant rows,
boot set) for as long as a Process handle or attachment names it, only so its
status word can be read. `MAX_PROCESSES` is small. Split the process in two:

- **The Process record**, a small entry in its own table, sized well above the
  body table. It holds what must outlive the process:
  - the generation (handle words name the record);
  - the state and the status word (`exit_kind`, `exit_code`);
  - the quota ledger (used and limits);
  - `refs` (Process handle entries and the attachment) and the attachment;
  - the final trap counters, so that `Stats` on a `Gone` process still
    answers. Today they are a row of the stats region keyed by slot (§2.7's
    "Stats reads the slot's row of the stats region, which survives with it").
    Decide whether the row is keyed by record or the counters are copied into
    the record at death, and say which in your report;
  - a field for the pid a later unit adds (the pid issue, SO-49). Mention only
    that the record is where per-process identity lives; don't specify pids.
- **The Process body**, an entry in the existing `MAX_PROCESSES`-sized table
  (donatable, `SlabKind.Processes`). It holds what only a live process needs:
  the handle table, the boot set, the protection domain and grant rows, entry
  and stack, and the thread bookkeeping. **The body is freed at teardown**, so
  a `Gone` process holds a record and no body.
- `ProcessCreate` takes one record and one body; either table full answers
  `NoResource`, policy first as §2.9 says for the other kinds. A record is
  reclaimed when its `refs` reaches zero and it is `Gone`.
- **Sizing:** the record table is sized so that `Gone` records held by handles
  and attachments don't starve creation: say how it is sized relative to the
  body table (a named constant; give the rule, not a number) and whether it is
  donatable like the other slabs (a new `SlabKind`, or growing with
  `SlabKind.Processes`). Decide; if it is not clear, ask.
- Handles name the record, so a handle to a `Gone` process stays valid and
  reads the status word; ops that need a body (`Map`, `Give`, `Start`, the
  creation ops, `BootHandleNext`) on a `Gone` process answer as §2.10 already
  rules (`Revoked`, or `BadState` for `Start`/`Give`; keep those rulings).

**Lead, SO-54.p1 (spec only): the Process record and the Process body.** This implements the user's split ruling and the later disown update; the user is reviewing the design in parallel.

**Model.**
- **The record** is small and outlives the process. It holds the state, the status word, the ledger, `refs`, the attachment and the final trap counters. Handles, attachments and charged objects name it. It is reclaimed when it is `Gone` with zero refs, whoever drops the last reference, which keeps SO-56's rule.
- **The body** is the existing `MAX_PROCESSES` table: handle table, boot set, domain and grants, entry and stack, threads. It is freed at teardown.
- **`ProcessCreate`** takes one of each, and either table being full answers `NoResource`.

**Implementer decisions.**
- Trap counters are copied into the record at death; the stats region stays body-keyed.
- `MAX_PROCESS_RECORDS = PROCESS_RECORDS_PER_BODY × MAX_PROCESSES`. This is a floor, not a guarantee.
- A new donatable `SlabKind.ProcessRecords`.
- No record generation, since handle entries carry generations.
- The `MAX_ATTACHMENTS` process term becomes records.

Everything unbuilt carries a "not yet enforced" marker. This revision is rebased over SO-56, with its reclaim rule kept in §2.7, §2.10 and §8. No line is over 80 columns, and there are no new unresolved § references.

**1. The record generation the ruling lists was dropped (~1072-1078, ~1218).** The brief's record holds "the generation (handle words name the record)". The patch leaves it out, saying "Handle generations (§3) make the reuse safe". That covers the names that hold a reference: Process handle entries, attachments and staged refs, all counted, so a record can't be reused under them. It doesn't cover the names that hold none. I had the tree censused, and these can name a record after it is reclaimed and reused:
- **`MappingSlot.target`, reachable today.** A husk keeps `target` with `row = NO_ROW` after its target dies, and that slot can be reused. If the target model's "a husk answers `Revoked`" is built by reading the target record's state, a reused record makes the husk look live, and `Unmap` would remove a grant from, and credit, the new occupant's domain and ledger.

Shared objects (`Memory`, `IoMemory`, `Pipe`) are covered by SO-53's disown. `LAST_PROT_PROCESS` and `MappingSlot.process` are covered too.

Either extend the disown to every survivor (write `NO_PROCESS` at the teardown into each object that outlives the death, the same shape as SO-53), or keep a record generation and store it beside each such field. **User rulings (Nov 9) on Air's r2 item 1 and the design decisions.**
1. **Disown, with no record generation.** A teardown writes `NO_PROCESS` into every object that outlives the death and names the dying record: `Exited` threads, invalid bound objects, Events, given Interrupts, a Mapping's `target`, and the record's own body index.   `objects.saw` (the ledger, `credit_shared`), `limits.saw`, and the
  `SlabKind`/slab-donation code (`dispatch.saw` `slab_donate`,
  `slab_geometry`, the install for each kind).

## The change

1. **Two tables.**
   - `PROCESS_RECORDS`: a new slab of process records, `MAX_PROCESS_RECORDS =
     PROCESS_RECORDS_PER_BODY * MAX_PROCESSES`. Pick the factor, generous and
     greater than one; the lead suggests 4. Name the constant; never quote its
     value in prose.
   - `PROCESSES`: the existing table, now of bodies.
2. **Fields, as the spec assigns them.**
   - **Record:** state, `exit_kind`/`exit_code`, `quota_used`/`quota_limit`,
     `refs`, `attachment`, the final trap counters (three `UInt64`), and a
     `body` index, `NO_PROCESS` (or a body sentinel) when it has none.
   - **Body:** handle table, boot set with count and cursor, grant record,
     entry/stack/region/rw fields, and anything else only a live process uses.
     Add a back-link to its record if the code needs one.
3. **The process number is the record index.** Every `process` field
   (threads, objects, `MappingSlot.process`/`target`, `calling_process()`) and
   every Process handle's target names the record. Code that needs the live
   parts goes through `record.body`.
   - There are about 120 `PROCESSES[` sites. Go through every one and decide
     record or body; a helper such as `body_of(p)` that faults the kernel on a
     record with no body keeps the sites honest.
   - Root is record 0 and body 0.
4. **Stats.**
   - While a process lives, its counters are its body's row of the stats
     region, keyed by body index, as today.
   - At teardown they are copied into the record.
   - `ProcessOp.Stats` on a `Gone` process reads the record's copy; on a live
     one, the body row.
   - A mapped stats-region reader sees the row until a create reuses the body;
     the spec says so already.
5. **Create.** `ProcessCreate` and the boot path take one record and one body.
   Either table full answers `NoResource`, policy first, with nothing taken. A
   partial find takes neither.
6. **Teardown,** in spec §2.10's numbered order. After the reap's disown, copy
   the counters into the record, clear the domain, drop the boot set, clear
   the ledger, and **free the body**, clearing `record.body` (part of the
   disown rule).
   - A Mapping whose `target` is the dying record gets `target = NO_PROCESS`
     (the husk); check every reader of `target` still behaves.
   - **A record is never reclaimed before its own teardown has ended.** Today
     an early reclaim is tolerated because it only writes the state; with the
     record split the teardown writes the record after that point, so enforce
     it. Either the teardown holds a reference on its own record until the end
     (the shape SO-53 r1 used: `ref_object` after `Gone`, the ordinary drop at
     the end), or a state that `reclaim_process_slot` refuses until the
     teardown ends. Pick one, say why, and remove the "sound only because"
     comment the code carries today.
   - The reap's process scan (SO-56) and every reclaim now reclaim records.
7. **Donation.** Add `SlabKind.ProcessRecords`, the next number after
   `Processes`, with its geometry and install, following the one-array
   `Processes` shape. Body donation stays `SlabKind.Processes`, still capped by
   `process_slots_usable()`. The records' cap is the slab capacity alone.
8. **Limits.** The `MAX_ATTACHMENTS` sum's process term becomes
   `MAX_PROCESS_RECORDS`, and any `static_assert` that used `MAX_PROCESSES` for
   something now record-keyed changes with it.
9. **The typed tier and ABI.** If sysapi exposes `SlabKind` (slab donation),
   add `ProcessRecords` there and to any C header/floor that lists the kinds.
10. **Spec.** Remove every "not yet enforced" marker this unit satisfies:
    - the one-`ProcessSlot` markers;
    - the Stats copy;
    - the record and body slabs;
    - the record table;
    - the `MAX_ATTACHMENTS` term;
    - the body freed at teardown;
    - the record's body link;
    - the husk's `target`;
    - root as the first of each table;
    - never reclaimed before its own teardown ends.
    
    Leave every marker that belongs to invalidation, the bound-kind disown, or
    the other lifecycle units. Edit a marker down to what is still unbuilt where
    it mixes the two.

## Tests

`gone_reclaim_teardown` and the `slab_*` donation cases:
1. **Dead processes don't hold bodies.**
   - Root creates and runs a child to exit and keeps its handle. It repeats this
