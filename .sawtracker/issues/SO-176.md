## Build

1. **`tests/net-client`.** One test package whose scenario is a build input
   (`netcase`), given per image by its `inputs`, as `tests/net-ping`'s
   `pingconfig` is. Each scenario attaches, does its one hostile or edge
   step, prints what it sees, and ends by itself. It exits nonzero on any
   failure path, so no case reaches the harness timeout.
2. **The rest of §7.3's table,** on riscv32, riscv32-flat and arm64, each
   with its `tests/images/net_*.toml`:
   - `net_link_down_first`, `net_tx_length`;
   - `net_ring_index_past`, `net_ring_index_order`, `net_ring_length_past`,

     `net_protocol_states`;
   - `net_slow_client`, `net_client_dies`, `net_wait_abandoned`.

   Leave out any row A3-4 already covers, and say which.
3. **`net_attach_handle`.** The design's request carries "an inlet sibling
   of its own". Root gives every client inlet `Post | Transfer` with no
   `Mint`, and carrying the inlet being called through is `BadArg`. So
   carry something else, as the reviewer suggested: a `Memory` cut from the
   client's heap pool. `HEAP_POOL_KEEP` includes `Transfer`.
4. **The two cases A3-4's review handed here:**
   - **`Attach` answering `NoResource`** (the driver reports it as the status
     `NoMemory` and keeps the connection). Give an image `[limits]` that
     leaves the Memory slab exactly full at the ring's allocation, beside
     `net_attach_too_large`. If the builder or kernel makes that exact fit
     impossible to construct, say why and don't force it.
   - **Receive buffers posted at `Attach`** (mutant M07). Write a peer
     script that speaks first, and a client scenario that only listens.
     QEMU queues the frame until a buffer is posted, so a driver that posts
     none at `Attach` leaves the client hanging. Expected: the client
     receives the frame.
5. **The peer's scripts.** `burst` and any others §7.3 needs, plus the
   speak-first script, in `tools/netpeer` with its hosted tests.

## Ordering and time

- No case asserts host time (CLAUDE.md). Every ordering is forced by
  construction: by a wait on the other side's action, never a delay.
  `net_wait_abandoned`'s yield ordering relies on the image arming no tick,
  as §7.3 explains. Check that this still holds under the priority
  scheduler's levels: a yield at one level, and a wake that preempts only a
  lower level.

  `PeerClosed` line before root stops") is stale. Root stops once every

**The implementer's note for SO-176.p1 (A3-5).**

**Cases.** Each runs on riscv32, riscv32-flat and arm64, and each ran twice with no flake.
- **`net_link_down_first`:** `set_link off` before the guest runs. The first event is `LinkDown`, the harness raises the link when the client prints "waiting for link", and a ping is then answered.
- **`net_tx_length`:** frames of 2000 and 10 bytes give `tx_errors=2`. The peer (`log`) sees only the stack's frames.
- **`net_ring_index_past`, `net_ring_index_order`, `net_ring_length_past`, `net_ring_tail_past`:** each gives `connection ended: … ring_faults=1` and `root: net ended status=65538`. The client's next call answers `PeerClosed`.
- **`net_attach_too_large`:** `Attach(256,256)` answers `NoMemory`, then `Attach(8,8)` and a ping succeed.
- **`net_attach_handle`:** a request carrying a heap share answers `BadOp`, then a plain `Attach` succeeds.
- **`net_ring_no_pin`, `net_ring_no_unpin`:** two cases, because the client faults on its first call. Each shows the `AccessDenied` fault, then the driver's `client gone` with its unpin.
- **`net_protocol_states`:** `BadOp`, `NotAttached` ×4, `BadArg` (zero slots), `Attached`, `Busy`, and `BadArg` for `Tx` and `RxReturn` naming slot 8 of an 8-slot ring.
- **`net_slow_client`:** peer script `burst`. All 20 datagrams arrive in order, with `rx_dropped=0`.
- **`net_client_dies`:** the client exits with its RX buffers posted.
- **`net_wait_abandoned`:** a yield with no tick, then the claim is dropped. The next `WaitEvent` is answered, and `abandoned=1`.
- **`net_greeted`:** the peer speaks first (`greet`) and the client only listens. This is the M07 pin.

**Mutants**, each alone on riscv32, all red:

| mutant | red case |
|---|---|
| config-change link read ignored | `net_link_down_first` |
| frame-length checks, `len < least` and `len > most` | `net_tx_length` |
| pktring `poll` without `IndexPastCount` | `net_ring_index_past` |
| pktring `poll` without `IndexOutOfOrder` | `net_ring_index_order` |
| pktring `take` without `LengthPastSlot` | `net_ring_length_past` |
| pktring `read_out_tail` without `IndexPastCount` | `net_ring_tail_past` |
| the `Tx` and `RxReturn` doorbell range checks, `NotAttached`, `Attached`, `Busy` | `net_protocol_states` |

**Not built.** `Attach`'s `NoResource` can't be reached at the gate:
- The builder sizes every slab to the sum of the quota rows, so a client within its quota meets `QuotaExceeded` first.
- An image can't lower the driver's quota below its declaration.

So `refusal()`'s `QuotaExceeded` and `NoResource` arms have no gate pin; its `NoMemory` arm does.

**Where the code differs from the design:**
- **The greeting.** A frame sent at connect is lost, because QEMU drops what it flushes to a device that isn't up. So `greet` blocks on stdin until the harness writes a line, which it does when "SOS netclient: attached" appears. This is ordered by an observable, with no clock.
- **Runner hooks.** New `before_cont` and `on_console` hooks (a QMP command, or a line to the peer, fired once on a console line). The peer is started with a stdin pipe.
- **Hang reports.** A hung case now reports the console and the peer's log.

**Expected texts.**
- Root's `root: client ended` is never asserted.
- `net_client_dies` asserts its counts from ` tx=2` on, since `rx=` depends on when the ARP reply lands.
- `net_protocol_states` stops at `abandoned=`, since abandonment races the client's exit.
- In the ring-fault cases, the client's `PeerClosed` line is asserted after the driver's exit line and before `root: net ended`, on the no-preempt rule. **Reviewer, please check this ordering argument.**

**Lock.** One early QEMU run (`--case net_greeted`) ran outside the suite lock. Every later run took it.

**Totals:**
- `make sos-test`: 1641 passed.
- `make sos-smoke-esp32s3`: 519 passed, 6 board-only not run.
- netpeer's `blade test` passes.

Approved patch SO-176.p1 (revision 1)

**Approved.** A3-5, with both of SO-173's hand-ons settled. I checked it on d65456c9 with sawlang v0.24.6.

**M07 is pinned.** With `post_rx` removed from `Attach`:
- `net_greeted` is red on riscv32 and arm64, and `net_ping` stays green on both. Its transmit completions post the buffers, which is why it couldn't pin M07.
- The client only listens: it waits for `RxReady` and reads one frame, and sends nothing, so nothing else posts a buffer.
- The greeting's order is an observable: `greet` writes only when the harness gives it a line, and the harness does that when `SOS netclient: attached` appears. The driver posts the buffers before it answers `Attach`.

**`refusal()`'s two unpinned arms hold up as unreachable.**
- **`NoResource`:** the builder settles every slab to at least root's own objects plus the sum of the children's rows (`sum_kind`/`derive`). An image stating less is refused, and a child's Memory need is the larger of its heaps and its row (`add_rows`). So a driver within its declared quota meets `QuotaExceeded` before any slab fills, and its handle table's capacity follows its quota.
- **`QuotaExceeded`:** still SO-173's argument, one `Attach` per life within the declared rows.
- **`NoMemory`:** with it dropped from `refusal`, `net_attach_too_large` is red on riscv32 and arm64.

**The ring-fault ordering you asked about holds, by construction.**
1. On a ring fault, `start` removes the outlet from the Waiter before `finish` prints and exits.
2. The outlet's last reference goes, and the kernel drops the staged `Stats` message (`pipe_drop_staged`) and answers the parked caller `PeerClosed` (`notify_claims`). That readies the client then, while the driver is still running.
3. A wake doesn't preempt and the image arms no tick, so the driver runs on through its lines and its exit. Its teardown then readies root (`notify_ready(Process)`), behind the client on the one level's ready list.

So the client's line falls after the driver's exit line and before `root: net ended`. The case comment gives only the first half (after the exit). Steps 1 and 2, which put it ahead of root, are worth a sentence there.

**The runner's hooks fail loudly:**
- A QMP command's error reply raises `ToolError` and fails the case.
- A trigger line that never appears leaves the guest waiting, which is a reported hang, now with the console and the peer's log.
- A write to a peer that has gone raises.
- A `greet` peer never given its line ends as "netpeer did not end when QEMU did".
