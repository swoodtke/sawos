## The contract (spec.md; grep for each)

- §2.8: `SystemOp.InterruptBind` / `SystemRight.InterruptBind`. A bound line
  answers `LineBound`, and the kernel's own line or a missing line is
  `BadArg`. A full Interrupt slab answers `NoResource` with no quota asked,
  and there is at most one live Interrupt per line.
- §2.9: no `Interrupt` quota column.
- §2.10:
  - the Interrupt row: one name, its handle;

- §3: `interrupt_rights()` has `Transfer` and no `Mint`, and an Interrupt has a

- §12's boot-set rights.

## The change

1. **Bind moves to System.**
   - Add `SystemOp.InterruptBind` and `SystemRight.InterruptBind`. Remove
     `ProcessOp.InterruptBind` and `ProcessRight.InterruptBind`, and renumber
     only if the op and rights tables require it (say what you did).
   - Faults land on the caller. The old "faults the named process" path, and
     its stranded-caller comment, go away for this op.
   - Typed tier: `System.interrupt_bind(line:)` replaces
     `Process.interrupt_bind(line:)`.
   - Update every caller in `tests/` and `root/` (grep `interrupt_bind`), and
     the System handle masks children receive, so drivers that bind still can.
2. **No quota.** Remove `QuotaKind.Interrupt`, its default limit and every
   charge and credit for it. A full Interrupt slab answers `NoResource`, policy
   first being moot here; §2.9's exception already says so. Check the ledger
   width and indices after removing a column.
3. **Rights:** `interrupt_rights()` drops `Mint` and gains `Transfer`.
   Attenuation is monotonic, so no sibling can be minted. Check the typed tier
   has no `Interrupt.mint` (SO-60 skipped it).
4. **Givable,** in SO-58's shape:
   - `BootHandleKind.Interrupt`, `msg_kind_of`;
   - typed `PipeHandle` case, carrier, `decode_handle`;
   - `Process.give(interrupt:, tag:, keep:)` with the hand-back;
   - the `BootHandle` accessor.
5. **One name, its handle.**
   - An Interrupt lives exactly as long as its one handle. Releasing it,
     explicitly or by its holder's teardown close-all, **destroys** it: unbind,
     mask the line, and make the line bindable again.
   - A give or a staged message moves the name; that is not a release.
   - An attachment does **not** extend its life. When the handle is released
     while an attachment holds it, the Interrupt becomes **invalid**:
     unbound, masked, line free. The attachment holds the invalid slot until it
     is removed, and fires as an invalidated waitable, a terminal level.
   - Its record's payload is `hal.IRQ_NONE`, never the old line
     (`waitable_answer`).
   - An invalid Interrupt with no references left is freed.
   - **Remove the by-owner Interrupt sweep.** An Interrupt has no owner
     lifetime; its handle's release is the destruction. Keep whatever SO-61's
     detach logic still needs.
   - Disown per §2.10: the bound-by `process` field goes to `NO_PROCESS` when
     the binder dies, or drop the field if nothing needs it; say which.
6. **No consuming attach.**
   - `WaiterOp.Add` with the consuming flag on an Interrupt target is a
     `BadArg` fault.
   - Remove `Waiter.give(interrupt:)` from the typed tier; fix any caller.
   - `Waiter.add(interrupt:)` borrows, as today.
7. **§9's flow:** where a test has a driver child bind its own line
   (`tests/child-echo-*`, `tests/svc-uart-*`, `tests/uart-*`; grep), move to
   root binding the line and giving the Interrupt to the driver, at least for
   one representative driver pair, and say which. If moving all of them is
   mechanical, do all of them.
8. **Spec:** remove every Interrupt marker this satisfies; edit mixed markers
   down to what remains. No issue numbers; 80 columns.

## Tests (probe first where the old behaviour is observable)

1. **Bind through System:**
   - `System.interrupt_bind` works;
   - a System handle without `InterruptBind` faults `AccessDenied`;
   - a bound line answers `LineBound`;
   - the kernel's timer line is `BadArg`.
2. **Give and send:** an Interrupt bound by root and given or sent to a driver
   child is usable there (`Wait`, `Ack`). A refused give hands it back.
3. **Release destroys:** releasing the handle unbinds; the line can be bound
   again at once.
4. **Holder's death destroys:** a driver holding the Interrupt dies; root can
   bind the line again.
5. **An attachment doesn't extend life:**
   - Attach an Interrupt to a Waiter, then release the handle.
   - The line is bindable again.
   - The attachment fires, and its record carries `IRQ_NONE`.
   - The level stays up until the attachment is removed by key.
6. **Consuming attach refused:** the raw consuming flag on an Interrupt is a
   `BadArg` fault.
7. **No `Mint`:** the raw `MINT_OP` on an Interrupt is the `AccessDenied` fault
   (no `Mint` right).

Mutation-check 3, 5 and 6.

**Lead review, SO-63.p1: interrupts bind through System, have one name and live as long as it.**

**Ops and rights.**
- `SystemOp.InterruptBind` and `SystemRight.InterruptBind` are added, and the Process op and right are removed.
- `ProcessOp` is renumbered dense, since numbers are not ABI. The `ProcessRight` bit is left unused.
- Faults land on the caller.
- Only root's System default carries the bind right, so drivers are given lines, not the right to bind.

**Quota.** `QuotaKind.Interrupt` is gone, and the ledger is now 9 columns. A full slab answers `NoResource`.

**Rights and givability.** `interrupt_rights()` loses `Mint` and gains `Transfer`. `BootHandleKind.Interrupt` is added, along with the typed give, carry, decode and accessor. `Waiter.give(interrupt:)` is removed, and a consuming attach on an Interrupt faults `BadArg`.

**Lifetime.**
- A new `InterruptState` (`Free`/`Live`/`Invalid`), and `InterruptSlot.process` is dropped.
- `interrupt_names` is `refs` minus the attachment's reference.
- When the names reach zero, `destroy_interrupt` masks the line, invalidates the slot, sets `line = IRQ_NONE` and notifies the attachment, a terminal level whose record carries `IRQ_NONE`. The state is re-read afterwards, since a one-shot delivery can free the slot.
- The by-owner sweep is gone; the teardown frees invalid Interrupts at zero. SO-61's revoke-first order is kept.
- **Please check the invariant `interrupt_names` relies on:** every attachment drop clears `attachment` before dropping its reference.

**§9's flow.**
- The `driver-child`, `uart-service` and `uart-cancel` families now have root bind the line and give the Interrupt to the driver.
- The line number is a board fact, linked per target through `tests/irq-lines/{riscv32,arm64}.c` via Blade's existing `native` sources.
- Root-as-driver tests use `system.interrupt_bind`.
- `irq-badline`'s `handles=` goes from 4 to 3, because it no longer derives a Process handle.

**`interrupts=`** now counts handle entries naming an Interrupt that the close-all destroyed. No asserted values changed.

**Probe (old kernel).** A consuming give was accepted, and an attached Interrupt kept its line bound after release (rebind → `LineBound`).

**Tests.** `irq_bind_denied`, `irq_bind_twice`, `irq_bind_timer_line`, `irq_consume_refused`, `irq_mint_denied`, `interrupt_attached_release`, `interrupt_give` and `give_interrupt_returns`. The mutants (destroy disabled, attachment counted as a name, consuming check off) are caught.

**Runs.** The full suite passes locally (529 runs), plus the C3 smoke.

Requested changes on patch SO-63.p1 (revision 1)

**Air: SO-63.p1, changes requested. The kernel is right; the peer-gone wake and the teardown free have no test that fails without them.**

**What checks out** (by reading 14ea0b3 + p1):
- **The `interrupt_names` invariant holds on every path that drops an attachment's reference.** Each clears the field before the drop: `waiter_remove`, the one-shot end in `deliver_attachment`, the Waiter free cascade and the attachment sweep. The rest drop none, or are pipe-only. Every name-dropping path (release, close-all, give, send, receive, `release_staged`, `release_carried`) runs the names check, and with no `Mint` names are 0 or 1, so destroy runs exactly when the last name goes. One caveat: `detach_attachment` silently no-ops when the row isn't on the list. SO-61's revoke-first order is what keeps every list intact.
- **Destroy's notify can't recreate the SO-61 double drop or mint into a dying table.** Every Waiter being torn down was revoked first, the wake reaches only intact lists and other processes' threads, and an Interrupt record mints nothing. The state and refs are re-read after the notify (refs.saw ~271-275).
- **The hardware side:**
  - `interrupt_by_line`, `alloc_interrupt` and `has_external_wake_source` skip Invalid;
  - the mask comes before the invalidation, and the free never masks a line that may already be rebound;
  - `IRQ_NONE` (0) is excluded by `irq_line_valid` on all three HALs;
  - nothing can fire between the mask and the invalidation.
- **The removed quota column:** every ledger access goes through `quota_column`, and `QUOTA_KINDS` = 9 = Pipe + 1. Optional: `static_assert(QUOTA_KINDS == (QuotaKind.Pipe as Int) + 1)`.
- **The `ProcessOp` renumbering:** sos.h's `Exit=2` and the .S payloads' `WaiterCreate=5` are unchanged, and ABI.md is updated. `irq-badline`'s handles 4→3 is justified.

**Asks:**
1. **The peer-gone wake on destroy is untested.** Deleting `notify_ready(WaitableKind.Interrupt, slot)` in `destroy_interrupt` (refs.saw ~443) passes every case.
   - `interrupt_attached_release` waits AFTER the release (irq-attached main.saw ~79), and `Wait`'s scan finds the Invalid level whether or not anyone was notified.
   - `interrupt_give` and the driver cases destroy into the holder's own Waiter, which `end_process` has already revoked, so the wake finds nobody.
   - That notify is the only thing that wakes a thread parked when the last name goes; without it, the thread is stranded.
   - The one-shot re-entry (the inner free during the notify, then the outer re-read) is also unexercised. Making the re-read just `refs == 0` (a double free), or moving `state = Invalid` after the notify (a recursive destroy), survives.
   - Suggested case: root binds, attaches to its own Waiter, gives the Interrupt to a child, and parks on the Waiter. The child exits holding it, and root must wake with its key and `line=0`. Run it once Persistent and once OneShot.
2. **The teardown's Interrupt sweep is untested.** Deleting the Invalid-at-zero free (sched.saw ~610-622) leaks each slot a dying holder leaves, and nothing notices: in `interrupt_give` the child's two land in slots 0-1, and root's rebinds go to 2-3 of a 4-slot slab. Suggested: after the child's death in irq-give, print `count_free` (as irq-attached does) before rebinding.
3. **A child that is never started pins a given line forever (suspected).** The mechanism isn't new, but this patch makes it reachable for lines. `reclaim_process_record` reclaims only `Gone` records, and nothing ends a `Created` child. If `start` is refused after `give(interrupt:)` (thread quota, a full slab) and the launcher drops the child, every rebind of the line is `LineBound`. The new §9 flow (bind, give, start) walks into it on a start refusal. Please add a spec known-limits line, or decide how a never-started child is reclaimed.
4. **Docs:**
   - ops.saw ~158: "`ProcessCreate` is the one factory on this object" (InterruptBind now creates too).
   - refs.saw ~567-574: the `free_object` docstring still credits against `process` and omits Interrupt from the no-credit list; ~505-506 says the Interrupt "belongs" to a process; ~436-437 "nothing after this call reads it" (the caller re-reads).
   - waiter.saw ~145-149: "reports it once … and keeps reporting it" contradicts itself.
   - system.saw ~349-350, ops.saw ~192-194, spec §2.8 and dispatch.saw ~1464-1466 say the bind "charges no quota row". `mint_handle` charges the caller's Handle row and answers `QuotaExceeded` at the budget; this matters for a non-root binder.
   - Optional: `interrupt_names` (waitables.saw ~185-189) would underflow a UInt16 if the invariant broke; a `fatal_kernel` there names it.

**Mutants:**
- caught: an attachment counted as a name; destroy disabled; the consuming check off; `IRQ_NONE` not written; `Mint` left in; the bind right unchecked; the `interrupts=` count;
- survive: the notify skipped (ask 1); the second re-read removed (ask 1); the sweep removed (ask 2); the line not masked at destroy (needs a raising device; already admitted).
