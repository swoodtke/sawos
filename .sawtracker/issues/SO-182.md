# Networking A5b: TCP data transfer, retransmission and RTO, hooked into the Stack

The networking design is `SO:networking`, §6.1 and §10's track A. A5a has
merged, as "netstack: TCP connection setup and teardown". It gives you:
- `drivers/netstack/src/tcp.saw`: the wire format, `Header`, `Received`,
  `write_segment` and `TcpDrop`;
- `drivers/netstack/src/tcpconn.saw`: the states, a `Table` of 8 slots with
  generation-carrying `ConnId`s, a `SegmentSink` and an `IsnSource`.

Data there is counted and acknowledged, never delivered. Read SO-181's notes
for what A5a left to this unit.

## Build (hosted, in `drivers/netstack`)

1. **Sending data:**
   - a send buffer per connection;
   - segmenting to the peer's MSS (with a floor on a tiny MSS);
   - the send window (`SND.WND`), and `SND.NXT` / `SND.UNA`;
   - PSH set on the last segment of a write.
2. **Receiving data:**
   - a receive buffer per connection, delivered in order;
   - the advertised window shrinks as data waits and reopens as the caller
     reads, following RFC 9293 §3.8.6's window management. Avoid silly
     window syndrome per RFC 9293 §3.8.6.2.
3. **Retransmission (RFC 6298):**
   - SRTT and RTTVAR, an RTO clamped to the RFC's 1 s minimum (as a
     constant you can lower in tests), and exponential backoff;
   - Karn's algorithm, so a retransmitted segment gives no RTT sample;
   - retransmitting the SYN, the SYN,ACK and the FIN;
   - a connection given up after a bounded number of tries.

   The clock is the caller's `now`, as everywhere in netstack.
4. **SYN-RECEIVED reclaim:** a SYN,ACK that's never answered after its
   retries frees the slot. That closes the forged-SYN fill A5a named.
5. **The Stack hook:**
   - the TCP table lives in `Stack`;
   - the IPv4 demux's `Tcp` arm feeds it, counting each `TcpDrop`;
   - segments go out through `send_ipv4`;
   - the API a caller uses: listen, connect, write, read, close, abort and
     poll(now).
6. **Out of scope:**
   - congestion control (A5c): keep the congestion window fixed at a
     generous value, or none, and say which;
   - the out-of-order queue, RFC 5961, keepalive, zero-window probes and
     the TIME-WAIT details (A5d).

   Out-of-order data stays dropped with a duplicate ACK.

**The implementer's note for SO-182.p1 (A5b: TCP data transfer, retransmission and the Stack hook).**

**Sending (`tcpconn.saw`).**
- Each connection has a 2048-byte ring (`SEND_BUFFER`). `write` answers the count taken, or `WouldBlock`.
- Segments are cut to the peer's MSS, between `MIN_SEND_MSS` 64 and 1460, inside the peer's window. `SND.MAX` is kept apart from `SND.NXT`.
- PSH goes on the segment that empties the buffer (RFC 9293 §3.9.1.2).
- The sender's silly-window rule and Nagle are in, overridden after `SWS_OVERRIDE_MS` 200.
- `close` queues the FIN behind the data. Data and the FIN wait in SYN-RECEIVED until the SYN is acknowledged.
- **There is no congestion control:** the peer's window is the only limit (A5c).

**Receiving.**
- Each connection has a 4096-byte ring. `read` answers 0 after the FIN once the buffer is empty, and `WouldBlock` before that.
- The window shrinks as data waits and never moves its right edge left. A read reopens it only by at least min(half the buffer, MSS), with an ACK (§3.8.6.2.2).
- Data past the window is trimmed, and a FIN behind it is not taken. Out-of-order data is dropped with a duplicate ACK.

**Retransmission (RFC 6298).**
- SRTT, RTTVAR and the RTO at 1 ms granularity, clamped to [min, `MAX_RTO_MS` 60 s]. A sample above the cap counts as the cap.
- `MIN_RTO_MS` is 1 s, and `Table(min_rto_ms:)` lets a test lower it.
- Karn: one segment is timed at a time, only new data at `SND.MAX`, and a timeout drops the measurement in progress.
- On expiry: the RTO doubles, `SND.NXT` goes back to `SND.UNA`, and only the earliest segment is resent.
- §5.7: a handshake that needed a retransmission starts its data at 3 s.
- A connection is given up after `SYN_RETRIES` 4 or `DATA_RETRIES` 8, and its id then answers `TimedOut`.
- **SYN-RECEIVED reclaim:** a SYN,ACK that's never answered frees its slot by the same give-up path, which closes the forged-SYN fill.

**Output the sink refuses.** `SegmentSink.send` returns a Result. A refused data, FIN, handshake or ACK segment stays owed and is sent next time. A refused RST, or an out-of-window ACK, is lost, as the network may lose one.

**Calls.**
- New: `accept`, `write`, `read`, `ephemeral_port`. `close` and `connect` take `now`, and `poll` takes a sink.
- New `CallError` cases: `WouldBlock`, `Listening`, `NotListening`, `Reset`, `TimedOut`. A finished id answers why it ended until its slot is reused.
- A listener that closes, aborts or becomes a connection aborts the connections it spawned and didn't hand out.

**The Stack hook (`lib.saw`).**
- The TCP arm feeds the table. A segment to a broadcast address or from 0.0.0.0 is `TcpDrop.NotUnicast` (RFC 1122 §4.2.3.10).
- `TcpCounts` counts each drop, read through `tcp_drops(reason)`.
- Segments go into a 2-segment outbox (`tcpout.saw`), which the Stack empties through `send_ipv4`, because the table can't send through the Stack while the Stack lends it.
- The API is `tcp_secret`, `tcp_listen`, `tcp_accept`, `tcp_connect`, `tcp_write`, `tcp_read`, `tcp_close`, `tcp_abort`, `tcp_state`, `tcp_status` and `tcp_drops`; `poll` runs the timers.
- **ISNs** follow RFC 6528: a 4 µs count plus SipHash-2-4 over the four-tuple (`isn.saw`).

**Decisions to review:**
- **The ISN key is the caller's.** The stack has no randomness, so it refuses to listen or connect (`OpenError.NoSecret`) until `tcp_secret` is called.
- **Memory:** the table is about 51 KB and the Stack about 61 KB. `tests/net-client` and `tests/net-ping` have arena 0x18000 and `WORK_STACK_WORDS` 65536.
- **Removed as unreachable:** the listener-generation check, a guard in `take_text` (now an invariant comment) and the Stack's port-0 check.
- **A5a goldens:** four were regenerated with independent Python, since ACKs after data carry the shrunk window.
- **The sealed-fields case** gained the new private fields.

**The peer:** a `tcp_echo` script on port 7 answers the SYN, echoes each data segment and answers the FIN. Every refusal has a hosted test.

**Cases.**
- Hosted: `tests/tcpdata.saw`, `tests/tcpstack.saw` (two stacks back to back) and `tests/isn.saw` (the SipHash vectors).
- The gate case `net_tcp_echo` (riscv32, flat, arm64) connects, writes, reads the echo back, closes to TIME-WAIT, then pings. Each step waits on the peer's frame for the step before. No time is asserted, and nothing is retransmitted.
- **119 of 119 mutants are red.**

**Left for later.**
- **A5c:** congestion control.
- **A5d:**
  - the out-of-order queue;
  - RFC 5961's challenge ACK;
  - keepalive;
  - zero-window probes, without which a lost window update leaves the connection stuck;
  - FIN-WAIT-2's timeout;
  - TIME-WAIT restarting on a repeated FIN.
- **Unassigned:** connections keep the old address after `configure` or `deconfigure`.

**Totals on A4's merge:** hosted netstack 11/11 and netpeer 4/4, gate 1716, S3 550.

- **Sequence arithmetic** is modular everywhere. My probe sends and receives data across 2^32 both ways, ACKs past the wrap included, and it passes.

- **The receive path:** the window's right edge never moves left, data past the window is trimmed, and the ring holds.
- **RFC 6298 and Karn** are followed, with all arithmetic in `UInt64`.
- **SipHash:** an independent implementation reproduces every vector in `tests/isn.saw` and `tests/tcpstack.saw`.
- **Owed segments** stay owed and are flushed.
- **The four regenerated A5a goldens** differ only by the shrunk window and its checksum.

An agent read it against RFC 9293, 6298 and 6528. I reproduced each finding below with a hosted probe in `drivers/netstack/tests` (the `tcpdata`/`tcpstack` rigs, sawlang v0.24.6).

**1. ✓ A connection in CLOSING with unsent data never sends it.** In `arrive`, the `Closing` arm returns when the FIN isn't acked yet, before `output`. Nothing else reaches `output` in CLOSING, since `write`/`close` answer `Closing`, `read` reopens only with text, and `tick` only on a timer.
- **Probe:** `connected(100, 8192)`, `write(250)` (two segments go and 50 bytes are held by Nagle), `close`, then B's FIN,ACK acking 1001, then B's ACK of 1201. Polling to 64 s sends nothing; the state stays CLOSING with `queued 50`.
- The 50 bytes and the FIN never go, and the peer waits in FIN-WAIT-2. RFC 9293 §3.10.7.4's "otherwise ignore the segment" ignores the segment, not the send queue.

**2. ✓ Comparing SND.NXT and SND.UNA with ISS breaks after 2^32 − 1 bytes** (`output`'s `snd_nxt == iss` → `send_syn`, `syn_acked = snd_una != iss`, `expire`'s `handshake = snd_una == iss`).
- When a segment or ACK boundary falls on ISS, the connection sends a SYN,ACK where its next data byte belongs, withholds data, and counts the handshake's retries.
- The peer picks ACK boundaries through its window, so it can aim at ISS. A long transfer also meets it by chance, at roughly one boundary per MSS per 4 GiB.
- Use a flag (SYN sent, SYN acked) rather than equality with ISS.
- **Probe** (about 90 s hosted): `connected(1460, 8192)`, then 2,941,758 writes of 1460 bytes, each ACKed, then 615 bytes ACKed, so SND.UNA = SND.NXT = ISS (1000).
  - A 1-byte write then sends nothing.
  - The timeouts send four SYN,ACKs (flags 0x12, MSS option) at seq 1000, and the connection ends CLOSED, its id answering `TimedOut`.

**3. ✓ A timeout's resend ignores the peer's window.** `Push.Resend` takes `unsent.min(mss)` and never `usable()`.
- **Probe:** `connected(1460, 100)`, `write(300)` (100 bytes go), then `poll(1000)`. One 300-byte segment is resent into a 100-byte window (RFC 9293 §3.8.6).

**4. ✓ A listener closed with three unaccepted children resets only two.** `abort_spawned` sends one RST per child into the 2-segment outbox and drops the rest with `let _ =`, and an ended child owes nothing.
- **Probe:** `Pair.up()`, three `tcp_connect`s to b:7, `pump`, `tcp_close(listener)`, `pump`. A's states are CLOSED, CLOSED, ESTABLISHED.
- `tcp_abort` of a listener goes the same way, and neither has a test with children.

**5. ✓ SYN,ACKs to an unresolved host take the whole ARP queue.** Only resets are marked as replies (`lib.saw`), so a SYN,ACK answering a possibly forged source takes the slot `arp.saw` keeps "always free for a datagram this station sends itself".
- **Probe:** `refused_segments`' four SYNs, sent to the listener on port 7 instead of closed port 9, leave `pending_frames()` 4 with no `ArpQueueFull` drop, against 3 and 1 for the resets. The SYN,ACK retransmissions keep refilling it.

**6. ✓ An RST or ACK after a go-back carries SND.NXT, not SND.MAX.** `expire` moves SND.NXT back to SND.UNA, and `send_ack`, `output`'s ACK and `reset` use it.
- **Probe:** `connected(100, 8192)`, `write(300)`, `poll(1000)`, `abort`. The RST's seq is 1101 while the peer's RCV.NXT is 1301, so the peer drops it as outside its window, and the abort doesn't reach it.

**7. ✓ An out-of-order segment that acks everything in flight sends nothing new.** `arrive` returns `OutOfOrder` after `take_ack` without `output`.
- **Probe:** `connected(100, 8192)`, `write(250)`, then B's 10 bytes at 9100 acking 1201. Only the duplicate ACK goes; the 50-byte tail, now free to go, waits for B's next in-order segment.

**8. The note overstates two things:**
- **"Data and the FIN wait in SYN-RECEIVED":** a close there with nothing queued sends its FIN at once. A5a's `closing_transitions` asserts that, and RFC 9293 §3.10.4 allows it, so correct the note and the doc, not the code.
- **"Closes the forged-SYN fill":** the reclaim frees a slot after `SYN_RETRIES`, 31 s at a 1 s RTO, so seven forged SYNs per 31 s still hold every slot, as `syn_received_reclaimed` itself shows. The module doc's "hold a slot only for the handshake's retries" is accurate. The note's claim isn't, so say what the reclaim does and doesn't bound.

**Smaller (say whether each is meant):**
- `SYN_RETRIES` 4 gives up an active open at 31 s, where RFC 9293 §3.8.3 asks R2 for a SYN to cover at least 3 minutes.
- `read` into an empty buffer answers `Ok(0)`, the end-of-stream answer, while data waits.
- `MIN_SEND_MSS` sends 64-byte segments to a peer that offered less, which is pinned.

- a write that straddles the send ring's index 2048;
- data across 2^32 on the send side (my probe covers it, and you're welcome to it);
