**Device objects: what the kernel knows about a device, and how drivers get, claim and map it.**

**From the owner and the reviewer (Nov 26).** Today a driver receives loose pieces:
- an `IoMemory` window, sometimes carved from a larger one;
- a separate interrupt line.

Nothing ties them to a device, and the kernel has no device records beyond base and length rows of the region table.

**The direction:** a Device object the kernel holds, handed to root to distribute. It carries the device's register ranges and interrupt lines, which can be retrieved, claimed and mapped.

**Source:**
- the image today: the board file, through the builder;
- later, a device tree, or bus enumeration (PCI, virtio).

**The design must settle:**
- the object and its ops;
- claims (exclusive ownership, and ending at release, give or death);
- sub-devices or carving, for slices of shared blocks such as the GPIO output-select used by the PWM driver;
- interrupts bound through the device;
- kernel-used devices (the console), replacing the interim console-claim system op of SO-165;
- bus enumeration in the kernel versus a userspace bus driver;
- the builder, stock root and driver migration.

**Order:** after the S3 UART driver (SO-165) and networking, unless the owner moves it.
